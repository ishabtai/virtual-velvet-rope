# Best-selling vending SKUs — Australia / Melbourne

Research date: 4 Nov 2026. Owner: Agent 04 (Product & Pricing).

> **Big caveat up front:** no public, independent ranking of Australian vending SKU sales was found. Searches returned supplier marketing, business-for-sale listings and US blogs. Everything below combines (a) the few Australian sources found, (b) Australian retail data on what convenience channels sell, and (c) clearly marked ESTIMATES from general industry practice. The founder should treat the first 8–12 weeks of telemetry (cashless/telemetry sales reports) as the real source of truth.

Jargon:
- **SKU** (stock keeping unit) = one specific product, e.g. "Coca-Cola Classic 600 ml PET".
- **Planogram** = the map of which SKU goes in which spiral/slot.
- **Velocity** = units sold per slot per week.
- **Telemetry** = the machine reports sales and stock levels over the internet (see Agent 05).

## 2. Probable top-sellers by category (ESTIMATE)

ESTIMATE reasoning: built from (1) brand dominance in Australian grocery/convenience (Coca-Cola, Pepsi/Schweppes, Mars, Nestlé, Cadbury/Mondelez, Arnott's/Smith's/PepsiCo snacks), (2) common SKUs seen in Australian operator planograms and business-for-sale listings, (3) slot formats that fit standard spiral machines. Rank order within a category is indicative only.

### Cold drinks (typically 40–60% of combo-machine revenue — ESTIMATE)
1. Coca-Cola Classic 600 ml PET
2. Coca-Cola No Sugar / Pepsi Max 600 ml
3. Mount Franklin (or house-brand) still water 600 ml
4. Red Bull 250 ml can / Monster 500 ml can (energy drinks — strong at night-shift, trades, gyms, students)
5. Sprite / Fanta 600 ml
6. Powerade / Gatorade 600 ml (gyms, sports clubs, warehouses in summer)
7. Iced coffee / flavoured milk (Farmers Union, Dare, Ice Break 500 ml) — **only in a refrigerated machine that holds dairy at ≤5 °C**; very popular in Victoria (Ice Break and Farmers Union are Australian convenience staples)
8. Sparkling water (Mount Franklin Lightly Sparkling, Capi) — growing in offices
9. Kombucha / low-sugar functional drinks (Remedy, Mojo) — offices, gyms, health services (GREEN/AMBER)
10. Coconut water / juice (Nudie, Golden Circle) — niche

### Snacks (ambient)
1. Smith's / Doritos / Red Rock Deli chips (45–50 g single serve)
2. Cadbury Dairy Milk 50 g, Mars, Snickers, Twix, KitKat, Maltesers single bags
3. Arnott's biscuits (Tim Tam twin pack, Shapes single serve)
4. Lolly bags (Allen's, Natural Confectionery Co., Starburst)
5. Muesli / "better-for-you" bars (Carman's, Uncle Tobys, Nice & Natural)
6. Nuts / trail mix (Cobs, Nobby's)
7. Protein bars (Musashi, Barebells, Ministry of Snacks, Snickers HiProtein) — high price point, gym/office
8. Cup noodles (Nissin, Indomie, Shin Ramyun) — **only where hot water exists** (break rooms, student housing, universities)
9. Jerky (Jack Link's) — trades sites, warehouses
10. Popcorn / rice crackers (Cobs, Sakata) — lower-calorie AMBER options

### Fresh / chilled (smart fridges, micro-markets — see Agent 05 for equipment)
- Sandwiches, wraps, salads, sushi, yoghurt pots (Chobani), Up&Go liquid breakfast, fruit cups, ready meals for microwave.
- Fresh is where office/healthcare demand is moving but carries **waste (spoilage) risk** of 5–15% of cost of goods — ESTIMATE.

### Coffee (bean-to-cup machines)
- Flat white, latte, cappuccino, long black dominate (Australian café culture). Melbourne consumers are coffee-literate and price-compare against $5–6 café coffee; a vending coffee must be good bean-to-cup with fresh milk or it will fail. ESTIMATE.

## 4. Rules of thumb for a starting planogram (ESTIMATE)

1. **80/20 rule:** in most machines ~20% of SKUs produce ~80% of revenue. Start with ~60–70% "core" proven sellers and 30–40% test slots, then cull the bottom 10% every 4–6 weeks.
2. **Duplicate the winners:** give the top 2–3 SKUs two spirals each so they never sell out (a sold-out slot = lost sale and annoyed customer).
3. **Eye level sells:** the middle rows (eye level ~1.2–1.6 m) are the premium slots — put highest-margin items there, not necessarily highest-volume.
4. **Price ladder:** offer an entry price (~$2.50–3.00), core ($3.50–4.50) and premium ($5–7) item in each machine.
5. **Local test:** ask the host site (office manager, gym owner) for a 5-item wishlist; it raises engagement and loyalty.
6. **Seasonality (Melbourne):** cold drinks spike Dec–Mar; hot drinks/coffee, noodles and chocolate stronger May–Sep. Chocolate melts in non-refrigerated machines in summer heat (Melbourne hits 40 °C+) — use refrigerated snack/combo machines or swap to non-chocolate lines in summer.
