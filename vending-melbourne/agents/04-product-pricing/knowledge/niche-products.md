## 1. Asian snacks & drinks
- **Evidence:** Melbourne wholesaler Asian Pantry reports vending operators and Melbourne university student associations as regular buyers of Asian snacks, drinks and cup noodles (supplier claim) — https://asianpantry.com.au/blogs/news/what-sells-in-vending-machines-recommendations-based-on-what-our-customers-keep-buying and https://asianpantry.com.au/ms/pages/vending-machines (search extracts, 4 Nov 2026).
- Comparable market: Japanese snack vending machines launched at Vancouver SkyTrain stations — https://www.richmond-news.com/food-and-drink/new-japanese-snack-vending-machines-skytrain-stations-richmond-burnaby-vancouver-bc-11155215
- Typical SKUs (ESTIMATE): Pocky, Hello Panda, Calbee chips, Shin Ramyun / Nissin cup noodles, Indomie, Kit Kat Japan flavours, Ramune, Calpis, Pocari Sweat, bubble-tea in a can, Lotte Milkis, Korean banana milk (chilled).
- Pricing (ESTIMATE): Pocky $3.50–4.00, Ramune $4.50–5.00, cup noodles $3.50–4.50, banana milk $4.00.
- Margin 50–65% (ESTIMATE) — novelty supports higher price.
- Risks: imported drinks must be CDS-registered and carry the Victorian refund mark; **use an Australian importer**. Food labelling (FSANZ English labels) — imports through reputable wholesalers already relabelled. Hand-off Agent 08.
- Positioning: a branded "Asian snack machine" (eye-catching wrap) is a differentiator for shopping centres and campuses (Agent 03, Agent 02).

## 2. Cup noodles / instant hot meals
- Requires a hot-water dispenser on site (many break rooms/student kitchens have Zip taps) or a machine with built-in hot water (Agent 05).
- Cost $0.90–1.80, vend $3.50–4.50 → 50–75% GM (ESTIMATE). Long shelf life (6–12 months) = low waste.

## 3. Protein / gym nutrition
- RTD shakes (Musashi, Chobani Fit, Up&Go Protein Energize — $3.60 retail for 500 ml, https://buywisely.com.au/product/up-go-protein-energize-vanilla-500ml), protein bars, electrolyte drinks, pre-workout (caution: high caffeine).
- 24/7 gyms (Anytime, Snap, Jetts, Plus Fitness) often run their own fridge; independent and boutique gyms are a better target (Agent 03).

## 4. Fresh food / smart fridges / micro-markets
- Smart fridge = locked fridge opened by card tap; weight sensors or AI vision charge for what you take. Micro-market = open shelves + self-checkout kiosk in a secure workplace.
- US benchmark: average 365 Retail Markets micro-market does 161 transactions/day, US$63,192/yr — https://365retailmarkets.com/blog/365-retail-markets-expands-micromarket-technology-australia (US data; 365 now in Australia).
- Fees example (US): Micromart US$60/month/unit + 4% + $0.10 per transaction — https://www.micromart.com/pricing/smart-fridge
- UK operator claims cashless baskets are >90% higher than cash ones — https://connectvending.co.uk/blog/the-rise-of-smart-fridges-in-workplaces/
- Australian vendor: The Vend Shop (Silverwater NSW / Tingalpa Qld) sells smart fridges — https://www.thevendshop.com.au/smart-fridges/
- **Key risks:** spoilage (5–15% of COGS, ESTIMATE), Victorian Food Act 1984 registration with the local council for perishable/potentially hazardous food (Agent 08), cold-chain logistics (Agent 06). Use long-life chilled lines (yoghurt, Up&Go, cheese & crackers, MAP-packed sandwiches with 5–7 day life) to reduce waste.
- Supplier options (ESTIMATE): commissary/catering kitchens and wholesale sandwich makers in Melbourne; Agent 06 to identify.

## 5. Frozen meals / ice cream
- Needs a frozen vending machine (−18 °C) — higher capex and power. Ice creams (Streets/Peters): sell $3.50–6.00, strong Dec–Mar, weak in winter (ESTIMATE).
- Frozen meals with on-site microwave (night-shift hospitals, warehouses): vend $9–14.

## 6. Hot food
- Automated pizza/hot-chip kiosks exist (e.g. NBR report "Hot chips from kiosks for Australians" — https://nbr.co.nz/hot-chips-from-kiosks-for-australians). Capex often $30k–100k+ per unit (ESTIMATE), food-safety registration Class 2, frequent cleaning. Hot food is always **GST-taxable** (ATO). **Not recommended for a first machine**; revisit after year 2.

## 7. Bean-to-cup coffee
- Gross margin 62–83% (see margins-by-category.md). Melbourne consumers benchmark against $5–6 café coffee; need quality beans (Melbourne roasters), fresh milk or premium milk powder, and weekly cleaning. Best in offices placed on a fee or service contract rather than pure commission (ESTIMATE).

## 8. Phone chargers, PPE, personal care
- **Chargers/cables/earbuds:** high ticket ($15–45), 50–70% GM, very low velocity — dedicate 2–4 wide slots, not a whole machine. Alternatives: power-bank rental networks (ChargeFUZE-style) — revenue share only.
- **PPE / industrial vending:** gloves, earplugs, safety glasses, hi-vis, batteries. Often run as *controlled-issue* vending where the employer pays and staff badge in (tracks consumption). Margin from service fee plus product mark-up 30–50% (ESTIMATE). Competes with industrial distributors (Blackwoods, Bunzl) who offer vending as part of supply contracts (Agent 02).
- **Personal care:** painkillers are Schedule/general-sale restricted; vending of medicines in Australia is limited — check with Agent 08 before stocking any medicine. Sanitary products, condoms, toothbrushes, deodorant are fine.

## 9. Recommendation for a beginner (ESTIMATE)
1. Start with a **mainstream combo (snack + drink) machine** in 1–3 good sites — proven products, quickest learning.
2. Add a **"Melbourne twist"** that competitors don't offer: an Asian-snack section (or dedicated machine at campuses/Asian-heavy suburbs) and a healthier, FoodChecker-compliant range for office/health sites.
3. Treat smart fridges/fresh as **phase 2** once you have a reliable route and a fresh supplier.
4. Avoid hot food, flowers, toys/claw as first ventures.
