# Healthy vending — Victorian Healthy Choices guidelines & planogram principles

Research date: 4 Nov 2026. Note: the official HEAS and health.vic.gov.au pages were **not fetchable** from this research session (network blocked); facts below come from search-result extracts of those official pages. Agent 08 should open the primary documents before any tender.

## 1. Why this matters commercially
- Hospitals, community health, many councils, universities, leisure centres and large employers now ask vending operators to prove "healthy" compliance. Without it, you are locked out of these locations.
- Public hospitals in Victoria are a **mandatory** market: every vending machine in a Victorian public health service must comply with the Healthy Choices policy directive.
- Compliance knowledge is a **differentiator** for a new operator pitching against incumbents (Agent 02).

## 2. The framework in one page

**Healthy Choices: food and drink classification guide** (Victorian Department of Health) classifies items with a traffic-light system:
- **GREEN — "best choices"**: e.g. plain water, plain/unflavoured milk, fruit, vegetables, wholegrain items, many unsalted nuts (serving size limits apply).
- **AMBER — "choose carefully"**: some nutritional value but moderate energy, saturated fat, sugar or sodium; e.g. many diet/no-sugar soft drinks in limited sizes, some flavoured milks, some muesli bars, baked crackers. ESTIMATE of examples — confirm each SKU in FoodChecker.
- **RED — "limit"**: low nutritional value, high energy/sat fat/sugar/salt; e.g. sugar-sweetened soft drinks, energy drinks, most confectionery, chocolate, regular potato chips. ESTIMATE of examples — confirm in FoodChecker.

**FoodChecker** — free online tool to rate any product as GREEN/AMBER/RED and run a "policy directive assessment" for vending machines: https://foodchecker.heas.health.vic.gov.au/how-to/criteria/

Supported by the **Healthy Eating Advisory Service (HEAS)**, run by Nutrition Australia Vic Division for the Department: https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices

## 3. Two levels of rules

### 3a. Healthy choices: policy guidelines for hospitals and health services (and the general guidelines for workplaces, sport & rec centres)
- Food: **at least 50% of food available/displayed GREEN, no more than 20% RED**. For combined food-and-drink machines, the percentages apply to the whole machine.
- Source: https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/government-policies-and-guidelines/healthy-choices-policy-guidelines-for-hospitals-and-health-services ; https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/workplaces-hospitals/healthy-choices-guidelines (accessed 4 Nov 2026 via search extract)
- The same guidelines are promoted (voluntarily) to workplaces and sport & recreation centres — councils running leisure centres often adopt them in tenders (ESTIMATE; Agent 03 to verify per council).

### 3b. Healthy choices: policy directive for Victorian public health services (mandatory)
- Applies to **all vending machines** at all sites of public health services (hospitals, community health centres, etc.), plus retail outlets and catering.
- Drinks: **no RED drinks** may be provided; the **majority of drink options should be GREEN**.
- Drinks requirements became mandatory statewide for vending and catering on **30 Sept 2022** (Monash Health news: https://coronavirus.monashhealth.org/?p=74058).
- Food targets were phased in, with the Victorian Cancer Plan 2020–24 aiming for ≥80% of hospital retail and vending meeting standards by 2024; directive updated **January 2025** (https://health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/healthy-choices-policy-directive-for-health-services-january-2025.docx), and a 2024 short summary says targets carry forward (https://health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/healthy-choices-policy-directive-for-victorian-public-health-services-short-summary2024.pdf).
- **Feb 2026:** Department reported all Victorian public health services achieved **100% compliance** for the second year running — https://health.vic.gov.au/news/victorian-public-health-services-hit-100-compliance-under-healthy-choices-policy
- Overview & targets PDFs: https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/HS_Healthy-choices-policy-directive-overview_Aug2024.pdf ; https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/HS_Healthy-choices-policy-directive-food-and-drinks-targets_Aug2024.pdf
- Publication hub: https://www.health.vic.gov.au/publications/healthy-choices-policy-directive-and-guidelines-for-health-services

### 3c. Schools
Victorian government schools follow the *School Canteens and Other School Food Services Policy*, which uses the same traffic-light system and restricts RED items (including in vending). **Not verified in this session** — hand-off to Agent 08. Commercially, schools are a poor target for a mainstream snack operator.

## 4. Official plan-o-grams
HEAS publishes example planograms for drink-only, snack-only and combination machines that meet the directive: https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices/healthy-vending/planograms and PDF https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/HS_Vending-Plan-o-Gram_Aug2024.pdf. ## 5. Practical compliant planogram (ESTIMATE — check every SKU in FoodChecker)

### Drinks machine, 40 selections (hospital — directive)
| Share | Classification | Example SKUs |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 50–60% | GREEN | Still water 600 ml (several brands/sizes), sparkling plain water, plain milk 300 ml, unflavoured soy/oat milk |
| ≤ 40–50% | AMBER | No-sugar soft drinks (Coke No Sugar, Pepsi Max) in ≤ 600 ml, reduced-sugar flavoured milk small size, kombucha, 99% juice small size, coconut water |
| 0% | RED | — (no regular soft drinks, energy drinks, sports drinks) |

### Snack machine, 40 selections (guidelines: ≥50% GREEN, ≤20% RED)
| Share | Classification | Example SKUs |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 50% (20 slots) | GREEN | Unsalted/lightly salted nuts 30–40 g, wholegrain crackers, plain popcorn, roasted chickpeas, fruit cups/fresh fruit (if refrigerated), some high-fibre bars |
| ≤ 30% (12 slots) | AMBER | Baked chips/crisps small pack, muesli bars, protein bars meeting criteria, dark chocolate small portion (check), rice crackers |
| ≤ 20% (8 slots) | RED | Small-portion chocolate, regular chips small pack |

### Merchandising rules of thumb (from Healthy Choices guidance, paraphrased; ESTIMATE where noted)
- Put GREEN items at **eye level and in the most visible slots**; RED items low/less prominent.
- **Price GREEN items competitively** — e.g. water cheapest drink in the machine. (Health services commonly do this; ESTIMATE.)
- No RED-item advertising on machine wraps; use water/fruit imagery.
- Smaller serve sizes for AMBER/RED (e.g. 375 ml, 30–45 g).

## 6. Commercial impact (ESTIMATE)
- Healthy machines usually see **lower unit volume (−10% to −30%)** versus a standard machine at the same site when first converted, because RED drinks/chocolate are the highest velocity items; partly recovered with higher prices on premium healthy lines (protein, kombucha, nuts).
- Gross margin % is similar or slightly lower (water is very high margin; nuts and protein lower).
- GREEN fresh items (fruit, yoghurt, salads) need refrigeration and add spoilage.
- **Advantage:** hospitals run 24/7 with staff, visitors and patients — high footfall and night-time captive demand. Contracts are usually let via tender to established operators; a newcomer may get in via **smaller private hospitals, aged care, community health and allied-health clinics** (Agent 03).

## 7. Checklist before pitching a "healthy" site
1. Ask which policy the site follows (directive, guidelines, own policy, none).
2. Run proposed planogram through FoodChecker; save the report as a PDF for the client.
3. Offer quarterly compliance audits as part of service.
4. Track sales by classification (GREEN/AMBER/RED) via telemetry and report to the host.
