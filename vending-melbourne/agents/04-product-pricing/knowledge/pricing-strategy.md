## 3. Pricing principles for a beginner

1. **Price in 50c steps.** Simplifies coin handling and looks clean; card makes 20c steps possible but customers prefer round numbers. Cash share in Australian vending is falling fast; most new operators go cashless-first (Agent 05).
2. **Good–better–best ladder.** Every machine should have an entry item (≤ $3), core items ($3.50–4.50) and premium items ($5+). Premium items lift average ticket even if they sell fewer units.
3. **Price the slot, not just the product.** If a slot sells 3 units/week, raising $0.50 costs little volume; if it sells 30, test carefully.
4. **Review every 3–6 months.** Wholesale has been rising (snack/confectionery ~6.5% yoy 2025, CDS container price up 31% Feb 2025). Small, regular increases (+$0.20–0.50) are better than one big jump.
5. **Never undercut by much where the host wants a staff perk.** Instead offer a *subsidised* price model: host pays a top-up per item or a monthly fee and staff pay less ("subsidised vending"). Converts a cost-sensitive site into a viable one.
6. **Commission vs price.** Sites that demand 20–25% commission force premium pricing; at standard prices you often can't afford >10–15% (see margins-by-category.md §3). Agent 03 should negotiate commission bands; Agent 07 model the break-even.
7. **Card surcharging.** Some operators add 20c for card (OzBargain report above). Australian rules (ACCC/RBA) cap surcharges at the cost of acceptance, and the RBA has been reviewing a ban on debit/credit surcharges — **status at Nov 2026 not verified** (hand-off Agent 08). Safer plan: build card cost into price; don't surcharge.
8. **Bundles / meal deals.** On smart fridges or app-enabled machines: "drink + snack $7" or "coffee + muffin". Lifts ticket ~20–30% (ESTIMATE).
9. **Dynamic/time-of-day pricing** (e.g. discount fresh food near use-by date) is possible on smart fridges with telemetry. Reduces waste. Agent 05 to confirm which platforms support it.
10. **Display and consumer law.** Show total GST-inclusive price clearly on each selection; refund procedure for failed vends must be posted (Australian Consumer Law guarantees) — Agent 08 to confirm wording.

## 4. Victorian Container Deposit Scheme (CDS Vic) — impact on drink pricing

What it is: from **1 November 2023** Victorians get a **10c refund** for returning eligible drink containers (most cans, PET and glass 150 ml–3 L; plain milk, wine/spirit bottles, cordial ≥1 L and some others excluded). The scheme is funded by beverage **first suppliers** (manufacturers/importers) paying a "container price" per container to the scheme coordinator VicReturn, which they pass into wholesale prices.
- Fact sheet: https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-08/Victorias%20Container%20deposit%20scheme%20factsheet%20accessible%20version.docx
- Reports page: https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/victorias-container-deposit-scheme-guidelines-and-reports

Key numbers (Recycling Victoria / Nous Group price-monitoring report, 2025, data Nov 2022–Oct 2024):
- Eligible **non-alcoholic** drink prices rose an average **9.1c** per container due to CDS; soft drink **9.4c**, water **5.1c**, juice 0.5c (not significant). Alcoholic +3.6c.
- Average supplier contribution was 12.3c/container → suppliers had **not** passed on the full cost.
- **1 Feb 2025:** average container price rose from **11.18c to 14.7c ex-GST** (+31%). Monitoring continued into 2025, so expect retail drink prices to have absorbed a further ~3–4c (ESTIMATE).
- Sources: https://vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/RV_Beverage-Price-Monitoring-Report_2025_Accessible.docx ; https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/Impact-of-Victoria%27s-Container-Deposit-Scheme-on-beverage-prices-and-consumption.docx ; media: https://www.3aw.com.au/soft-drink-price-rises-directly-linked-to-new-victorian-container-recycling-scheme/ ; https://www.packagingnews.com.au/sustainability/vicreturn-releases-price-for-new-cds

What it means for a vending operator (ESTIMATE):
1. **Cost impact is ~13–17c per drink (incl. GST) on top of pre-2023 cost** — roughly 3–8% of COGS on a drink. Pass it through; customers have already seen shelf prices rise.
2. **Don't itemise "10c deposit"** on the machine — the consumer price is just higher. (Unlike some overseas schemes, the refund is not a separately charged deposit at point of sale.)
3. **Opportunity:** offer a container collection bin next to machines and redeem the 10c refunds yourself or donate them to the host site's charity/club (a nice "green" pitch to offices, schools, sports clubs). With ~1,000 containers/month across a small route → ~$100/month (ESTIMATE). Check whether the host or the cleaners already do this.
4. **Imported drinks risk:** if you import Japanese/Korean drinks directly, you may become a **first supplier** obligated to register with VicReturn and pay container prices, and labels must carry the refund mark. Buy imported drinks through an Australian importer that already handles CDS (hand-off Agent 06/08).

## 5. Price elasticity notes (ESTIMATE)
- Captive, low-alternative sites (night shift, hospital after-hours): low elasticity — +10% price loses <5% volume.
- Offices within 200 m of a convenience store/supermarket: high elasticity — keep 600 ml at ≤ $4.50, emphasise convenience items (cold, instant).
- Students: very price-sensitive; use cans, noodles and entry-price lines.
