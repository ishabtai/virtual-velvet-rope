### A: Classic route (score 5)
- Checklist: FoodTrader Class 4 notification ($0) · baseline items · written licence agreement per site · RCM-marked machines bought from an Australian supplier (importing yourself makes you the EESS Responsible Supplier) · GST-inclusive single price on every selection · **no card surcharge** (networks may ban it from 1 Oct 2026) · refund log · exclude tobacco, vapes and alcohol.
- Extra cost over baseline: **~$0**. Outdoor or public-land sites add a council local-law permit (e.g. Merri-bek $380 application) plus an electrician's Certificate of Electrical Safety for any new outdoor circuit.
- Insurance: PL + products ($20m), machine theft and vandalism at third-party premises, vehicle cover for business use.

### B-lite / A + B-lite with fresh food (score 3)
- Checklist: **Class 3 annual registration** with the principal council · Statements of Trade for every machine location · buy sealed product only from licensed manufacturers (labels, allergens, use-by) · fridge ≤5°C with **telemetry, temperature alarms and auto-lockout** · temperature, cleaning and use-by records · recall procedure (supplier → batch → machine) · camera notice + short privacy policy, **no facial recognition** · GST tagging per SKU.
- **New finding: some councils charge per machine.** City of Casey's 2026-27 schedule lists "food vending machine registration (class 2 & 3)" at **$175 each** (https://www.casey.vic.gov.au/node/123, search extract). Other Class 3 fees: Maribyrnong $175, Banyule $107 (commercial), Glen Eira $194, Dandenong $307 (2025-26, ABLIS). Model **$175–$310 per machine per year** as the downside case, and $200–$450 per business as the base case. So 5 fridges could cost ~$900–$1,500/yr in council fees.
- Choosing the principal council is a cost lever: it is your business address or food-prep site.
- Insurance: add stock spoilage / machinery breakdown and consider product recall. Budget **$1,500–$3,500/yr** for 3–10 fridges, rising to $3,000–$6,000+ for a micro-market.
- Self-made food (Class 2): add a registered kitchen, a Food Safety Program (~$500–$2,000 if prepared by a consultant, ESTIMATE), Food Safety Supervisor training (~$150–$250), and Class 2 fees (~$400–$900/yr). **Recommendation: buy fresh food in, don't make it.**
- GST grey area: cold packaged food is generally GST-free and hot food is taxable. Whether a workplace fridge is supply "for consumption on the premises" (which would make food taxable) needs an accountant's view.

### C: Niche (score 3, spread 2–4)
- **Asian/imported snacks:** buy only from an Australian importer already registered with CDS Vic, with English labels and PEAL allergen panels. Direct import makes you a **CDS first supplier** (agreement, reporting, contributions) and the importer of record for labelling. Score 4 if bought locally, 2 if imported directly.
- **Hot food / pizza robots:** Class 2, FSP, FSS, daily cleaning, and probably a council inspection. Expect landlord fit-out rules. Score 2.
- **Gacha / capsule toys:** not food, but toys for children must meet the mandatory safety standard for small parts (children under 36 months) and the ACL product-safety regime (background knowledge, verify). Vending Times (Jun 2026) reports Australian concern about these machines. Score 3.
- **PPE/tools on industrial sites:** PPE sold as safety equipment must match its claimed standard (ACL misleading-conduct risk). Low burden. Score 4.

### D: Buy a route (score 3)
- One-off legal cost: solicitor's sale-of-business contract and due diligence **$2,500–$7,000**; accountant's financial due diligence **$1,500–$5,000** (ESTIMATE). Add PPSR searches (~$2–$4 each), a re-notification on FoodTrader under your entity, re-papering of host agreements, and possibly duty (no Victorian duty on a pure goodwill or business-asset transfer was confirmed; verify).
- See §3 for the due-diligence checklist.

### E: Franchise (score 2)
- One-off: franchise lawyer review of the disclosure document and agreement **$2,000–$5,000**, plus accountant review ~$1,000 (ESTIMATE). Annual: royalties or marketing levy (02 suggests modelling 6–12% of gross as the downside), plus the same food and insurance items as A or B. The franchisor may mandate your insurer or minimum cover (Provender listing: insurance $1,500–$5,000/yr).

### F: Placement package (score 2)
- One-off: solicitor review **$500–$1,500** before paying any deposit. Annual: as A.

### G: Locator (score 4)
- PL insurance (lower limit may suffice; ~$400–$800/yr, ESTIMATE). Written locator agreement with operators. If you take ongoing fees for a system you control, check that you are not operating a franchise.

## 3. Due-diligence checklist for buying a route (D)
1. **Entity and title.** Is it an asset sale (usual for small businesses) or a share sale? Search the **PPSR** against the seller's ABN/ACN and every machine serial number. Financed machines or reader rentals (e.g. Nayax) may belong to a financier.
2. **Site agreements, one by one.** Is there a written agreement for each site? Check the term, notice period, commission base, exclusivity, and above all **whether it can be assigned** or needs host consent. Make host consent (or a signed new agreement) a **condition precedent** for the top sites, which carry most of the revenue.
3. **Earnings verification.** Get 12–24 months of telemetry exports (Nayax or Cantaloupe), bank deposits, BAS lodgements and tax returns, and reconcile them. Misleading profitability claims breach the ACL (s18 and s151/s29 false representations), and the ACCC has pursued vending "distributorship" sellers before (Global Pre Paid: ~$35m compensation; https://accc.gov.au/media-release/35m-compensation-ordered-after-accc-stops-phone-card-scams). Get the earnings figures written into the contract as **warranties**.
4. **Inherited compliance.** Is the seller's FoodTrader notification or registration current, and at the right class (fresh lines = Class 3)? Are there test-and-tag records? Are the machines RCM-marked? Are any readers on 3G (closed in 2024)? Is there tobacco or vape stock (exclude it)?
5. **Liabilities that follow the business.** Check GST, PAYG and super debts, employee entitlements if staff transfer, and any open refund or injury claims. Get an indemnity and a **retention or escrow** from the price.
6. **Restraint of trade** on the seller: no competing in the same area for 2–3 years, and no soliciting the hosts. Cascading restraint clauses are common in Victoria; have a solicitor draft them.
7. **Handover.** The seller introduces you to every host, and you get a training period. Add a price adjustment (earn-out or clawback) if named sites leave within 6–12 months. 03 estimates 10–20% site loss after purchase.
8. **Insurance from settlement day**, and new merchant accounts in your entity's name.
9. **Is it really a franchise?** If the seller keeps supplying stock, a brand or a system for a fee, the Franchising Code may apply.

## 4. Red-flag contract terms

### Franchise (E), Franchising Code of Conduct (in force from 1 Apr 2025)
- The franchisor must give the **disclosure document, agreement and information statement** at least **14 days before signing**. You then have a **14-day cooling-off period** with refund of payments (minus reasonable expenses). The Key Facts Sheet requirement was **removed** from 1 Apr 2025. (Treasury key-changes table https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/p2025-633841-t.pdf ; Mondaq; LegalVision, search extracts.)
- Red flags: (1) **sites or national contracts held by the franchisor**, which leave with the franchisor when you exit (03, 05); (2) **post-term restraint** that blocks the "bootcamp then go independent" plan (H4); (3) **mandated suppliers** with an undisclosed margin, plus price cards set by the franchisor (04); (4) **merchant account, telemetry data and machine software owned by the franchisor**, so the machines stop working after you leave (05); (5) unilateral changes to the operations manual that impose new costs; (6) a territory that overlaps the franchisor's own machines (Ausbox both operates and licenses in the west, per 02); (7) earnings figures that are not in the disclosure document or are not backed by data; (8) termination for minor breaches, and no exit right for you if the territory doesn't perform; (9) marketing-fund levies without an annual audited statement.
- **Check whether a "licence" or "distribution package" is legally a franchise.** If it is, and no disclosure is offered, that is itself a red flag.

### Placement package (F), Australian Consumer Law
- Red flags: (1) **"guaranteed" or average earnings claims** without machine-level data (s18 misleading conduct; false profitability representations); (2) "secured sites" with **no written host agreement in your name**, or one that is not assignable to you; (3) sites the host can end on 30 days' notice, so you are paying a premium for a 12-month rolling site (03); (4) a re-site guarantee with an **undisclosed takings threshold** (Royal Vending's 180-day re-site, per 03); (5) a non-refundable deposit before you see the sites; (6) the seller keeping the Nayax contract or the commission flow; (7) machines without an RCM mark or an Australian warranty provider; (8) pressure tactics or "only 2 territories left".
- Protections: misleading conduct (ACL s18), unconscionable conduct, and unfair contract terms where the package contract is a standard form and you are a small business (penalties apply from Nov 2023). Pay by credit card where possible, and visit and count foot traffic at each site before paying.

## 5. Conflicts with other agents
1. **05 (G scored 4 for "rent-to-trial").** Fine on equipment, but a rented machine still needs Class 4 notification, PL insurance and a host agreement. G is light only for a pure locator.
2. **04 water GST.** 04 cites reg 38-3.01 for "GST-free beverages supplied from vending machines". In the current GST Regulations 2019 the provision appears to be **38-3.02** (the earlier 1999 version used 38-3.01). I could not read the operative text, so keep plain water as "generally GST-free, confirm with your tax agent".
3. **00 hybrid "B-lite in hospitals" and 01's A + B-lite.** Compatible on law, but fresh food lifts the **whole business** to Class 3. 07 should model per-machine fees in the downside case (Casey $175 each), not my earlier single-fee assumption.
4. **00's archetype E evidence lists "Ausbox licence package".** A licence package can be a franchise in law. Treat E and F as legally overlapping until the document says which it is.
5. **02's "model royalty 6–12%" is consistent** with my view that E should never be modelled with zero ongoing fees.
6. **Instant asset write-off.** My knowledge base said 2026-27 was unverified. Search extracts report the $20,000 threshold was made permanent from 1 Jul 2026 by a bill passed in Aug 2026, but Royal Assent was not confirmed. 07 can use $20,000 per asset as the base case, flagged.

## 6. What I verified this round (8 searches)
- **Franchising Code 2025:** 14-day disclosure and 14-day cooling-off confirmed; Key Facts Sheet removed (secondary sources + Treasury table).
- **Instant asset write-off:** $20k for 2025-26 is law; permanent from 1 Jul 2026 reported as passed in Aug 2026, with Royal Assent unconfirmed (ATO page title + Accountants Daily, search extracts).
- **Class 3 vending fees:** Casey 2026-27 $175 **per machine**; Maribyrnong $175, Banyule $107 (2025-26). This resolves open question 2 as "it depends on the council; some charge per machine".
- **Retail Leases Act 2003 (Vic):** no vending-specific guidance found. The VSBC has a "lease or licence" guideline. Leases under 12 months fall outside the Act unless occupation runs past 12 months. Still open (question 10); keep the "licence, not a lease" drafting.
- **GST vending beverages:** the provision is likely numbered 38-3.02 in the 2019 Regulations; its text was not read.
- **WorkCover:** the $7,500 threshold appears only in secondary sources; the 2026-27 average premium rate is held at 1.8%.
- **ACL business-opportunity enforcement:** ACCC Global Pre Paid (vending distributorships) and Duco Magic precedents found; no current penalty figures.
- **Route due diligence:** general Australian guidance (PPSR, tax debts, asset vs share sale) found; nothing vending-specific. The checklist in §3 is my synthesis.
- **Still unverified:** the Royal Assent of the instant asset write-off bill, the WorkSafe threshold on WorkSafe's own site, whether outdoor signage needs planning permission, and any Victorian duty on a business purchase.
