# Food Act 1984 (Vic): Vending Machines

> General information only, not legal advice. Your principal council's Environmental Health Officer (EHO) has the final say on how your business is classified. Research date: Nov 2026.
> Note: health.vic.gov.au and foodtrader.vic.gov.au could not be fetched directly in this session. The facts below come from search-engine extracts of those pages and from council listings on ABLIS (business.gov.au). Confirm them on FoodTrader before relying on them.

## 1. Key concepts (jargon explained)
- **Food Act 1984 (Vic):** the Victorian law under which every food business, including each vending machine selling food or drink, must be **registered or notified** with a council.
- **Classification (Class 1, 2, 3, 3A, 4):** risk tiers. A lower number means higher risk and more obligations. A vending machine's class depends on **what food it sells**, not on the machine itself.
- **FoodTrader:** Victoria's statewide online portal for registering, notifying and lodging statements of trade for vending machines and mobile or temporary food premises.
- **Principal council:** the council you register or notify with. For vending this is the council where you prepare the food for the machines **or** the council of your usual business address. If you are not based in Victoria, it is the council where you first operate. (Source: ABLIS council listings, see below.)
- **Statement of Trade (SoT):** a lodgement telling councils where and when your machines operate, so the council where each machine sits knows it is there. Required for Class 2 and 3. **Class 4 is exempt.**
- **Food Safety Program (FSP):** a written plan for controlling hazards. Required for Class 2 only.
- **Food Safety Supervisor (FSS):** a person with accredited food safety training. Required for Class 2. Class 3 premises do not need an FSS (since the 2022 reforms).
- **Potentially hazardous food (PHF):** food that must be kept at or below 5°C or at or above 60°C to stop bacteria growing, for example sandwiches with meat or dairy, salads, sushi, cut fruit and ready meals (background knowledge, Food Standards Code Standard 3.2.2; verify).

### Grey areas to confirm with your EHO
- **Coffee machines** (bean-to-cup with fresh milk): fresh milk is potentially hazardous, so a council may treat this as Class 2 or 3. Powdered-milk machines are lower risk.
- **Smart fridges with bought-in, sealed, chilled products** (yoghurt, sandwiches made by a licensed manufacturer): likely **Class 3**, since the FoodTrader example is "pre-packaged sandwiches". If **you** make the sandwiches, your kitchen needs its own registration (likely Class 2) as well.
- **Ice-cream freezers:** pre-packaged ice-cream is usually treated as low risk, but confirm whether your council treats it as Class 3 or 4.
- **Water and ice vending:** water refill stations may raise drinking-water quality questions with council.
- **Mixed machines:** the highest-risk item in the machine sets its class. A single sandwich line in an otherwise Class 4 machine can lift the whole business to Class 3.

## 4. Ongoing food safety duties (all classes)
Even Class 4 operators must comply with the **Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code**:
- **Labelling (Standard 1.2.1):** food sold from a vending machine has a specific rule. Labels **displayed in or on the machine** must state the **name and business address of the supplier** (the vending operator). Source: Standard 1.2.1, https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00386/2022-08-12/2022-08-12/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
  - Practical step: put a sticker on each machine with your business name, address and a contact phone or QR code. This also serves the ACL refund process.
- **Warning and advisory statements and allergens:** if any food is exempt from bearing a label (for example unpackaged food in a Class 2 machine), the required warning statements, such as allergen warnings, must be **displayed on or in connection with** the machine, for example on the machine front or a poster. Source: Allergen Bureau guide to Standard 1.2.3, https://allergenbureau.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/User-Guide-to-Standard-1.2.3.-Warning-and-advisory-statements.pdf
- **Pre-packaged stock:** only buy from legitimate suppliers who comply with labelling rules, including Plain English Allergen Labelling (PEAL, transition ended Feb 2026 per AFGC slides: https://afgc.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-Webinar-2-Food-Standards-Code-Labelling-and-other-information-requirements.pdf). Do **not** buy grey imports with non-English labels.
- **Best-before / use-by:** selling past **use-by** is illegal. Selling past best-before is legal only if the food is still fit, but that is poor practice. Rotate stock (FIFO).
- **Temperature control (Standard 3.2.2):** chilled potentially hazardous food at **≤5°C**. The machine needs a reliable thermostat, and ideally **telemetry with temperature alarms and auto-lockout** when the temperature goes out of range (background knowledge, verify; hand-off to agent 05).
- **Cleanliness and pests:** keep machines clean and keep cleaning records.
- **Recalls:** keep records (supplier, batch, which machines) so you can pull stock fast. Watch FSANZ recall notices.

## 5. Step-by-step for a new operator
1. Decide the product range, which determines the class.
2. Identify your principal council (business address or prep site).
3. Create a FoodTrader account (business details, ABN).
4. **Class 4:** submit a notification with the number of machines. Done, with no fee or renewal. Update it if your operation changes (the update process is not confirmed, see open-questions).
5. **Class 3:** apply for registration, pay the council fee, and lodge Statements of Trade for machine locations.
6. **Class 2:** prepare an FSP, appoint a trained FSS, register, lodge SoTs, and prepare for inspection.
7. Label every machine with the operator's name and address.
8. Keep records: suppliers, stock rotation, cleaning, and temperatures (chilled machines).

## 6. Related Victorian product rules
- **Tobacco vending machines:** from 1 Jul 2025, every premises with a tobacco vending machine must hold a Victorian tobacco **retail licence**. Unlicensed selling is an offence from 1 Feb 2026. The **person in charge of the premises** is responsible for stopping sales to under-18s. Source: https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/tobacco-vending-machine-factsheet.pdf . **Recommendation: exclude tobacco and vapes entirely** (vapes are pharmacy-only nationally from 2024, background knowledge).
- **Alcohol:** a liquor licence under the Liquor Control Reform Act 1998 would be required, and unattended alcohol vending is not practical. Exclude it.
- **Energy drinks and caffeine:** no Victorian age ban was found. Some schools and hospitals ban them under their own policies, so check host policies.
- **Schools:** Victorian government schools follow the Department of Education's healthy canteen policy, which uses a traffic-light (Healthy Eating Advisory Service) system that restricts "red" products such as sugary drinks and confectionery. This needs confirmation (hand-off to agent 03/04).
- **Hospitals and Victorian public-sector sites:** the Victorian *Healthy choices* policy guidelines apply to vending in public health services. Background knowledge, needs verification (hand-off to agent 04).
