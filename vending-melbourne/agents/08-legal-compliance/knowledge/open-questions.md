## B. Hand-offs to other agents
- **00 Lead Strategist:** compliance burden by model, from easiest to hardest:
  1. Class 4 snack/drink machines (free statewide notification)
  2. Class 3 pre-packaged sandwiches/pies (annual registration plus Statements of Trade)
  3. Class 2 self-made fresh food or hot food (Food Safety Program, Food Safety Supervisor, inspection)
  4. Direct imports of machines or drinks (EESS and CDS first-supplier obligations)

  Tobacco, vapes and alcohol should be excluded.
- **01/04 Market & Product:** a single sandwich or pie line lifts a machine to Class 3, and fresh milk coffee may be Class 2. Imported specialty drinks trigger CDS first-supplier duties unless bought through a registered local importer. Check GST status for each SKU. Schools and hospitals apply healthy-choice rules.
- **02 Competitor Analyst / 09 Devil's Advocate:** be wary of "turnkey vending business opportunity" sellers. If it is a franchise, the Franchising Code requires a disclosure document 14+ days before signing. Check route sales for assignable host agreements.
- **03 Location Scout:** every site needs a written host agreement (template provided). Expect large hosts to impose their own contracts, $20m PL and tenders. Footpath or council land needs a local-law permit (e.g. Merri-bek $380 application). Hosts pay the electricity, so disclose kWh.
- **05 Equipment & Tech:** require **RCM-marked, EESS-registered** machines from an Australian supplier. Prefer drop sensors or guaranteed vend, automatic cashless refunds, temperature telemetry with lockout (for potentially hazardous food at ≤5°C), and anti-tilt brackets. Avoid facial recognition. Do **not** program card surcharges (networks can ban them from 1 Oct 2026).
- **06 Supply & Ops:** keep records of supplier/batch to machine (recalls), FIFO and use-by checks, cleaning logs, test-and-tag log (12-monthly assumed), and a refund log. Each machine needs an operator name and address label.
- **07 Financial Modeller:** placeholders, all ESTIMATE unless cited:
  - ASIC company $636; annual review $342/yr; business name $47 (1 yr) or $108 (3 yr)
  - Class 4 $0; Class 3 ~$200–$450/yr; Class 2 ~$400–$900/yr plus FSS training ~$200
  - Insurance $700–$1,500/yr (1–3 machines), $1,500–$3,500 (5–15)
  - Test and tag $5–$15/item/yr
  - Solicitor review of the host template $500–$1,500
  - Accountant $500–$4,000/yr depending on structure
  - Commission 0–20% of net sales (US benchmark; large hosts 25%+)
  - GST: confectionery, chips and soft drinks are taxable; plain water and plain milk are generally GST-free
  - Company tax 25% (base rate entity)

## C. Possible staleness flags
- ASIC fees: indexed each 1 July (figures here are from 1 Jul 2026).
- Card surcharging: new regime from 1 Oct 2026, so network rules may still be bedding in.
- Tobacco licensing: new regime enforced from 1 Feb 2026.
- Privacy reform: in progress.
- Council fees: 2025–26 figures quoted; 2026–27 may differ.

## D. Phase 2b updates (4 Nov 2026, see `../analysis/phase2-analysis.md` §6)
- **Q2 partly resolved:** fees depend on the council, and some charge per machine. City of Casey 2026-27 lists "food vending machine registration (class 2 & 3)" at $175 **each** (https://www.casey.vic.gov.au/node/123). Maribyrnong Class 3 $175 and Banyule $107 commercial (2025-26, ABLIS).

- **Q7 note:** the vending-beverage GST provision appears to be reg 38-3.02 of the GST Regulations 2019 (F2019L00417); its operative text was not read.
- **Q10 still open:** no vending-specific Retail Leases Act guidance found. The VSBC "lease or licence" guideline is the starting point.
