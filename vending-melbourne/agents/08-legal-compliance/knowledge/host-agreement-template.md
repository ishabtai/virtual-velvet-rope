# Host (Location / Site) Agreement: Essentials and Template

> General information only, not legal advice. This is a **starting framework**, not a finished contract. Have a Victorian solicitor review it before use (ESTIMATE: $500–$1,500 for a one-off review of a reusable template). Research date: Nov 2026.

## 1. Why you need a written agreement
- A vending business is **two separate legal relationships**: (a) operator ↔ host site, and (b) operator ↔ machine supplier or financier (Sprintlaw: https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/legal-checklist-for-owning-and-operating-vending-machines-in-australia).
- Without a written agreement, a host can ask you to remove the machine at very short notice (Sprintlaw: https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/starting-a-vending-machine-business-in-australia-legal-steps-and-compliance/). Your machine investment then has no secured revenue.
- A written agreement is also the asset a buyer pays for when you **sell the route**. Agreements that cannot be assigned, or that terminate on 7 days' notice, make a route worth much less.

## 2. Unfair contract terms (UCT): this applies to YOUR template
- From **9 November 2023**, it is illegal to propose, use or rely on unfair terms in **standard-form contracts** with consumers or **small businesses**. Small business here means fewer than 100 employees or turnover under $10m. Many cafés, gyms and offices that host machines qualify.
- Maximum penalties are $50m for companies and $2.5m for individuals (company figure as reported).
- Source: https://www.smallbusiness.nsw.gov.au/news/new-unfair-contract-penalties-win-small-business ; https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/businesses-urged-to-remove-unfair-contract-terms-ahead-of-law-changes
- **Implications:**
  - If **you** issue a take-it-or-leave-it template to small hosts, avoid one-sided terms. Examples are terminating whenever you like while the host is locked in, changing commission unilaterally, or very long auto-renewals with heavy break fees.
  - If **you** are the small business signing a large host's standard form (a university, hospital or shopping-centre manager), UCT protections can help **you**.
- Balanced terms are both lawful and easier to sell to hosts.

## 3. Commission benchmarks
- Commission is usually a negotiated **percentage of gross sales**. Some small sites take no commission and see the machine as an amenity (Vending Machines Australia: https://vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/vending-machine-business-australia).
- US guides put the range at **5–20% of gross sales**, by site type and volume (https://vendingexchange.com/vending-contracts/commissions-in-vending-contracts). **No Australian survey data was found.** Treat this as an ESTIMATE range.
- **Define the base:** gross *excluding GST*, net of refunds and card fees? Ambiguity here is the most common dispute (ESTIMATE, based on how agreements commonly go wrong).
- **GST on commission:** if the host is GST-registered, the commission is a taxable supply by the host to you, so the host should issue a tax invoice and you can claim the GST credit. Consider using a **recipient-created tax invoice (RCTI)** agreement, where you generate the invoice from telemetry data. This is background knowledge; confirm with your accountant (hand-off to agent 07).
- Alternatives: a fixed monthly fee, tiered commission (e.g. 0% below $X per month, 10% above), or a free-vend or subsidy model where the employer pays for staff product.

## 4. Template: key clauses (fill in the brackets)

```
VENDING MACHINE PLACEMENT AGREEMENT

Parties
  Operator: [Entity name], ABN [ ], [address]   ("Operator")
  Host:     [Entity name], ABN [ ], [site address] ("Host")

1. Grant of licence
   1.1 Host grants Operator a non-exclusive licence (not a lease) to install, operate,
       service and remove the Equipment at the Location shown in Schedule 1.
   1.2 This agreement does not create a tenancy. [Note: a licence avoids retail-lease
       legislation complexity; confirm with solicitor that the Retail Leases Act 2003 (Vic)
       is not triggered.]

2. Term
   2.1 Initial term: [12/24/36] months from Installation Date.
   2.2 Renewal: automatically renews for successive [12]-month periods unless either
       party gives [60] days' written notice before the end of the then-current term.

3. Equipment and ownership
   3.1 The Equipment remains the sole property of Operator (or its financier) at all
       times. Host acquires no interest in it and must not sell, encumber or move it.
   3.2 Operator may replace, upgrade or reconfigure the Equipment by notice.
   [Note: consider PPSR registration for high-value machines; see section 5.]

4. Exclusivity (optional)
   4.1 During the term Host will not permit any other party to install [snack / drink /
       fresh-food] vending machines or smart fridges at the Location.

5. Location, power and access
   5.1 Host provides: floor space as per Schedule 1; a dedicated [10A/15A] general
       power outlet within [1.5] m; [Wi-Fi or mobile coverage]; electricity at Host cost.
   5.2 Access: Operator may access the Location during [hours] for restocking,
       cleaning, cash collection, maintenance, and with [24] hours' notice otherwise.
   5.3 Host will not relocate the Equipment without Operator consent, except in an
       emergency.

6. Operator obligations
   6.1 Stock, price, service and clean the Equipment at Operator's cost.
   6.2 Comply with all laws including the Food Act 1984 (Vic), the Food Standards Code,
       the Australian Consumer Law, and electrical safety requirements (test and tag).
   6.3 Respond to faults reported by Host within [2] business days.
   6.4 Provide a refund mechanism for customers (display phone / QR on machine).
   6.5 [Product range: Host may request reasonable exclusions, e.g. no energy drinks,
       or healthy-choice percentages; Operator will comply where commercially reasonable.]

7. Host obligations
   7.1 Take reasonable care of the Equipment; notify Operator promptly of damage,
       faults, vandalism or power interruptions.
   7.2 Not tamper with, unplug (except in emergency) or open the Equipment.

8. Commission
   8.1 Operator pays Host [ ]% of Net Sales each [month/quarter], within [14] days of
       period end, with a sales statement per machine.
   8.2 "Net Sales" means gross receipts from the Equipment less GST, refunds given,
       and [payment-processing fees].
   8.3 [Minimum sales threshold: no commission is payable for any period in which Net
       Sales are below $[ ].]
   8.4 GST: if Host is registered for GST, amounts are GST-exclusive and Operator pays GST
       on receipt of a tax invoice [or: parties agree to RCTI arrangement].
   8.5 Host may audit sales records on [10] business days' notice, once per year.

9. Insurance and liability
   9.1 Operator maintains public & products liability insurance of at least $[20]m and
       provides a certificate of currency on request.
   9.2 Each party is liable for, and indemnifies the other against, loss to the extent
       caused by its own negligence, breach or wilful misconduct (proportionate indemnity).
   9.3 Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss.
   9.4 Operator bears the risk of loss/damage to Equipment except where caused by
       Host's negligence.

10. Termination
   10.1 Either party may terminate on [60/90] days' notice after the initial term [or:
        at any time on 90 days' notice].
   10.2 Either party may terminate immediately if the other commits a material breach
        not remedied within [14] days of notice, or becomes insolvent.
   10.3 Operator may terminate on [30] days' notice if Net Sales are below $[ ] per
        month for [3] consecutive months (unprofitability clause).
   10.4 On termination, Operator removes the Equipment within [10] business days;
        Host gives reasonable access. Final commission paid within [30] days.

11. Assignment
   11.1 Operator may assign or novate this agreement to a purchaser of its vending
        business on written notice to Host. [Important for route resale value.]
   11.2 Host must procure that any purchaser or new occupant of the Location is
        notified of this agreement [best-endeavours].

12. Privacy
   12.1 Operator will handle any personal information (e.g. loyalty app, refund
        requests, camera images in smart fridges) in accordance with applicable privacy
        law and its privacy policy.

13. Disputes
   13.1 Parties will first negotiate in good faith for [10] business days, then may
        refer to mediation via the Victorian Small Business Commission before litigation.

14. General
   Governing law: Victoria. Notices in writing (email permitted). Entire agreement.
   Variation only in writing signed by both parties.

Schedule 1: Location, machine type/serial, power, access hours, commission rate,
            photos at installation.
Signed by both parties (authorised signatories).
```

## 5. Notes on specific clauses
- **Licence vs lease:** describing the arrangement as a *licence* to occupy a small area, with no exclusive possession, helps avoid the *Retail Leases Act 2003 (Vic)*. This is background knowledge and **must be confirmed by a solicitor**, especially for shopping centres.
- **PPSR (Personal Property Securities Register):** if a host goes insolvent, an administrator may treat a machine on the host's premises as the host's asset. Registering your interest on the PPSR (a small online fee) can protect you where the agreement amounts to a "PPS lease" (e.g. serial-numbered goods held for more than a year). Background knowledge; confirm with a solicitor.
- **Electricity cost:** a cold-drink machine uses roughly 3–8 kWh per day (ESTIMATE, hand-off to agent 05). That is about $300–$800+ a year at Melbourne retail tariffs, which is a real cost to hosts and a frequent reason they demand commission or ask for removal. Be transparent about it.
- **Large hosts** (universities, hospitals, councils, Westfield/Vicinity-type centres, train stations) use **their own contracts** and often run tenders. Expect licence fees or high commissions (25%+, ESTIMATE), detailed KPIs, healthy-choice policies and $20m insurance (hand-off to agent 03).
- **Council or public land:** a machine on a footpath or council land needs a council local-law permit (see `consumer-law-and-safety.md` §6). Never place one there under a private host agreement alone.

## 6. Pre-installation checklist per site
- [ ] Agreement signed by an authorised person (check they actually control the space; a tenant may need landlord consent).
- [ ] Certificate of currency provided.
- [ ] Power outlet tested; machine tested and tagged.
- [ ] Location photographed; safe access path; anti-tilt bracket installed.
- [ ] Operator name and address label and refund contact displayed on the machine.
- [ ] Machine included in FoodTrader notification or registration and Statement of Trade (Class 2/3).
- [ ] Host told how to report faults.
