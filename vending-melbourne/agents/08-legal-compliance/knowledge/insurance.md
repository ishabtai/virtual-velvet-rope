# Insurance for a Melbourne Vending Operator

> General information only, not financial or legal advice. Insurance is a financial product, so get a quote and read the Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) through a licensed broker. Research date: Nov 2026.

## 1. Why insurance is non-negotiable
- **Hosts and councils require it.** Councils that permit goods on public land commonly require **$10m–$20m public liability**. Merri-bek requires a $10m minimum and Cardinia a $20m minimum (ABLIS listings: https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/permit-to-place-or-display-goods-in-a-public-place-merri-bek-city-council/40884 ; https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/permit-to-place-or-display-goods-in-a-public-place-cardinia-shire-council/40838). Corporate, university, hospital and shopping-centre hosts usually require **$20m** and a **certificate of currency** before installation (ESTIMATE, based on typical facility-management requirements).
- **Sole traders have unlimited liability** (see `business-structure-registration.md`).
- **Typical incidents:** a machine tips onto a person (people rock machines to free stuck items), an electrical fire, a slip on a spilt drink, food poisoning or an allergic reaction, a vandal breaking glass, theft of cash, a refrigeration failure spoiling stock.

## 2. Cover types

| Cover | What it covers | Priority | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public liability (PL)** | Injury to third parties or damage to their property caused by your business, e.g. a machine falls on someone or a leak damages the host's floor | **Essential** | Combined PL + products for small businesses is about **$370–$840/yr**, typical ~$605/yr (Upcover, Aug 2026: https://www.upcover.com/blog/public-liability-insurance-cost-australia). A $20m limit costs more than $5m or $10m, but not proportionally more |
| **Products liability** | Injury or illness caused by products you sell (food poisoning, allergen, foreign object). Usually bundled with PL as "public and products liability" | **Essential** | Included above |
| **Machine / equipment cover** (often "general property", "portable equipment" or "business contents at specified locations") | Theft, vandalism, fire, storm or water damage to machines in **third-party premises** | High, especially outdoor and 24/7 sites | ESTIMATE: 1–3% of insured value per year, so about $100–$300/yr per $10k machine. Get quotes; many standard packs cover only your own premises |
| **Machinery breakdown** | Compressor or electronics failure | Medium (often a warranty matter instead) | ESTIMATE $50–$150/yr per machine |
| **Stock deterioration / spoilage** | Chilled or frozen stock lost through breakdown or power outage | Medium for smart fridges, low for snacks | Often an add-on to machinery breakdown |
| **Money / cash in transit** | Theft of cash from machines or while being transported | Low if cashless-first, medium for coin machines | Add-on |
| **Motor vehicle (commercial use)** | Van or ute used for restocking. A private policy may not cover business use | Essential if driving for business | Check with your current insurer |
| **Business interruption** | Lost profit after an insured event | Low at start | Add-on |
| **Product recall** | Cost of pulling a product | Low for resellers of branded stock; higher if you make your own food | Specialist |
| **WorkCover (WorkSafe Victoria)** | Employee injury, compulsory once you have workers above the wage threshold | Required once you hire | Premium is a percentage of wages |
| **Personal accident / income protection** | Covers the founder (not covered by WorkCover as a sole trader) | Recommended for owner-operators who lift stock | Varies |

US benchmark (not AUD and not directly comparable): vending operators average about US$442/yr for general liability (https://www.insureon.com/retail-business-insurance/vending-machine-operators/cost).

## 4. What to tell the insurer (or the claim may be refused)
- Business description: "vending machine operator: pre-packaged snacks and beverages placed at third-party premises" (plus fresh food or coffee if relevant).
- Number of machines, their value and their locations (indoor or outdoor, 24/7 or business hours).
- Product types (Class 4 only, or chilled or fresh food).
- Whether you **import machines yourself**. You may then be treated as the importer or manufacturer for product liability, and the machine must be electrically compliant (see `consumer-law-and-safety.md`).
- Annual turnover, plus cash versus cashless share.
- Any food you make yourself.

## 5. Contract interaction (host agreements)
- Hosts often ask to be named as an **"interested party"** or **"additional insured"** on your PL policy. Check that your policy allows this.
- Watch for **indemnity clauses**. An agreement saying you indemnify the host "for any loss however caused" (including the host's own negligence) may go beyond what your policy covers, because insurers often exclude "contractually assumed liability". Negotiate a **proportionate** indemnity instead: each party is responsible for losses to the extent it caused them (see `host-agreement-template.md`).
- Keep a **certificate of currency** PDF ready, because sites ask for it before installation.

## 6. Risk controls that reduce premiums and claims
- **Anchor or anti-tilt brackets** on tall machines, plus warning stickers ("Do not rock or tilt").
- Test-and-tag records (see `consumer-law-and-safety.md`).
- Telemetry with temperature alarms (fridges) and door-open alerts.
- Cashless-only or low-cash machines.
- CCTV coverage at the host site (the host's cameras), with privacy considerations noted.
- Photograph each machine at installation, documenting its condition and the site.

## 7. Where to get quotes
Use a broker experienced in retail or food (found via NIBA, the National Insurance Brokers Association) or online small-business platforms (e.g. Upcover, BizCover). Compare at least three quotes. Check the **exclusions**: unattended premises, theft without forcible entry, food-borne illness, and imported products.
