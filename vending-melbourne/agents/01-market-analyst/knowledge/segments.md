# Customer Segments — Attractiveness Assessment (Melbourne)

*Agent 01 Market Analyst · Research date: 4 Nov 2026. Ratings are **ESTIMATES** built from the evidence in industry-overview.md, melbourne-demographics.md and consumer-trends.md. Agent 03 (locations) and Agent 07 (finance) should test them.*

**Definitions:**
- A **segment** is a type of site/customer, e.g. "warehouses with 100+ staff".
- **Captive** means users have few alternatives nearby.
- **Dwell** means people wait or linger on site.

---

## 1. Scoring framework

Each segment is scored from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) on:
1. **Demand:** foot traffic × captivity × hours.
2. **Accessibility for a newcomer:** how easy it is to win the site without a track record or tender capacity.
3. **Margin potential:** pricing power vs commission demanded.
4. **Growth:** whether the segment is expanding in Melbourne.
5. **Operational ease:** theft and vandalism risk, access hours, distance and parking.

Total out of 25.

## 3. Segment profiles (top picks)

### S1: Industrial / logistics shift sites (★ TOP PICK)
- **Who:** warehouses in Truganina, Laverton North, Derrimut, Dandenong South, Campbellfield and Tullamarine; manufacturing in Dandenong and Clayton.
- **Why:**
  - 24/7 shifts, roughly 450k shift workers metro-wide (ESTIMATE).
  - Few nearby food options.
  - The western logistics belt is growing fast with population.
  - Site managers want a "staff amenity" at no cost to them.
  - Products: energy drinks, soft drinks, water, chips, chocolate, and meals and pies in fridges.
- **Watch:**
  - Some big DCs already have national-operator contracts.
  - Cash is still used (keep cash acceptance).
  - Night-time theft risk is lower in staff-only areas.
- **ESTIMATE revenue:** A$800–2,500 per machine per month at a 150-staff, 2-shift site (above the A$650–750 "average" in public sources).

### S2: Mid-size workplace micro-markets / smart fridges
- **Why:**
  - Hybrid-era employers are adding food perks to bring staff back.
  - Smart fridges sell fresh meals at A$8–12 baskets.
  - Low theft risk because staff are identifiable.
- **Watch:**
  - Established competitors: Morsl, Provender (franchise), Agora Markets.
  - Fresh food needs food-safety compliance and waste control (Agents 06/08).
- **Revenue driver:** subsidy agreements, where the employer pays a fixed fee or tops up prices.

### S3: Student accommodation and apartment towers
- **Why:**
  - About 322k international enrolments in Victoria.
  - Large PBSA operators (Scape, Unilodge, Iglu, etc.; verify with Agent 02/03) and 300–600-unit towers in Southbank, the CBD and Docklands.
  - Users are nearly 100% cashless.
  - Late-night demand.
  - Asian snack ranges command premium prices.
- **Watch:**
  - Semester seasonality (Dec–Feb dip).
  - Federal caps on international student numbers.
  - Owners' corporation approval processes.

### S4 / S8: Health
- **Private** health (S4): more accessible, often less strictly regulated.
- **Public** health (S8): the highest demand, but:
  - mandatory Healthy Choices compliance;
  - procurement often through tenders or bundled food-service contracts;
  - very hard as a first site.
- **Build a Healthy Choices-compliant planogram anyway.** It is reusable for councils, gyms and schools, and is a differentiator.

### S5: Gyms, leisure centres, sports
- **Why:**
  - Growth corridors are opening new council leisure centres.
  - Gym-goers buy protein, water and electrolyte drinks at premium prices.
- **Watch:** council centres may apply Healthy Choices guidelines, and some gyms run their own retail counters.

## 4. Under-served / growing segments (opportunity flags)

1. **Western logistics belt (Wyndham/Melton/Brimbank):** fastest population and DC growth. **ESTIMATE:** fewer established operators per site than inner suburbs.
2. **Healthy Choices-compliant vending:** a regulatory requirement creates demand for operators who know the traffic-light system. **ESTIMATE:** many small operators do not.
3. **Fresh-food smart fridges at 100–300-staff industrial sites:** a gap between "chips machine" and a full canteen.
4. **Residential/PBSA vending:** under-used in Australia compared with Asia.
5. **Employer-subsidised workplace pantries:** a B2B fixed-fee model that reduces revenue risk.

## 5. Segments to avoid at the start (for a zero-base founder)
- Public transport, shopping centres and street locations: tenders, high rents, vandalism.
- Universities and public hospitals: central contracts and procurement barriers. Revisit once there is a track record.
- Small CBD offices: hybrid work, café competition and low volume.
- Unvalidated novelty niches as the *only* strategy.
