## Top findings

1. **Market size is uncertain and the estimates conflict.** IMARC puts it at USD 428m (2024) and Ken Research at USD 3.5bn (2025).
   - ESTIMATE: A$1–3bn national gross vending/unattended-retail sales; Victoria about 25%.
   - Growth is a slow **~3–4% CAGR**: a mature, fragmented market (~185 "players" per Ken Research).
2. **Cashless is standard, but keep cash where it is used.**
   - The RBA 2025 survey puts cash at ~15% of payments, the first rise since 2007. Mobile wallets are up in all age groups.
   - Vending cashless share was ~60–85% in 2020. ESTIMATE: 75–90% at white-collar and student sites now.
3. **Melbourne is growing fast:** about 5.44m people (Jun 2025), +105k (+2%) in 2024–25, the largest gain of any capital. Growth is concentrated in the western, northern and south-eastern corridors, where the logistics and industrial precincts are.
4. **Best segment for a newcomer: industrial/logistics shift sites** (West: Truganina, Laverton N, Derrimut; South-east: Dandenong South; North: Campbellfield, Tullamarine). These are 24/7, captive and accessible. Next best:
   - mid-size workplace smart fridges and micro-markets;
   - student accommodation and apartment towers.
5. **CBD office vending is weak.** Melbourne CBD office vacancy is 19% (Jan 2026), the worst of any capital-city CBD, and about 36% of workers usually WFH.
6. **Healthcare is huge but regulated.** Victorian public health services employ about 157k people (headcount, Jun 2025), but the **Healthy Choices directive (Jan 2025)** requires ≥50% GREEN and ≤20% RED, with no sugary drinks in all their vending. Being able to offer a compliant range is a differentiator.
7. **Growth is in formats, not volume.** Smart fridges, micro-markets and employer-subsidised pantries are the growth area. The players are Morsl, Provender (franchise) and Agora; Coca-Cola launched AI smart coolers in 2024.
8. **Revenue benchmarks come from sellers and are low confidence.**
   - Public sources say ~A$650–750 gross per machine per month; a real Sydney listing works out at ≈A$725.
   - ESTIMATE: A$800–2,500/month at good industrial sites, and under A$250/month at small offices.
9. **Buying-group option:** the AVBA holds ACCC collective-bargaining protection until 23 Jun 2027. A small operator could get better supplier pricing through it.
