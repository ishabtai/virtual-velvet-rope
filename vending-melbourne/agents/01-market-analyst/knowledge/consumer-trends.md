## 1. Payments: cashless is the default, but cash is not dead

**RBA 2025 Consumer Payments Survey** (diary of about 1,200 people and about 14,000 transactions, Oct–Dec 2025; published Apr–May 2026):

| Finding | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cash share of payments (by **number**) | **~15%** in 2025, up from 13% in 2022: the **first rise since the survey began in 2007** | https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/apr/cash-use-in-australia-what-the-2025-consumer-payments-survey-tells-us.html |
| People using cash in a typical week | About half of Australians | Same / Canberra Times coverage |
| Heavy cash users | ~7% (down from 8%) | Same |
| Debit cards | ~half of all payments | RBA May 2026 Bulletin: https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/may/consumer-payment-behaviour-in-australia.html |
| Mobile wallets | Use rose across **all age groups** | Same |
| Small payments | Cards now dominate even small transactions that used to be mostly cash | Same |
| Demographics | Cash use is higher among older and regional Australians (sources differ on how much) | Canstar / SBS coverage |

**Vending-specific evidence:**
- Benleigh Vending (described as the largest independent operator in Australia) reported **~60% cashless** across its fleet, and **up to 85%** at sites with younger users (Nayax case study, c.2020; stale): https://www.nayax.com/hu/news/benleigh-vending-au-chooses-nayax-prepaid-solution-to-fight-covid-19/
- **ESTIMATE (Nov 2026):** vending cashless share in Melbourne is about **75–90%** at offices, universities, hospitals and apartments, and about **60–75%** at blue-collar or older-demographic sites. *Reasoning:* the 2020 baseline of 60–85%, plus six years of growth in mobile wallets. The RBA survey covers all retail, but vending tickets are small (A$3–6), where cards have overtaken cash.
- Industry belief (US-based, not verified for Australia): cashless raises the average ticket because people are not limited to the coins in their pocket. A widely quoted "+20–30%" claim comes from vendor marketing (smartbuy.alibaba.com). **Treat it as unverified.**

**Implications:**
1. Card/phone-wallet readers are non-negotiable on new machines.
2. Keep a coin/note mechanism on machines at industrial or older-worker sites. Cash has stabilised at ~15%, and losing those sales costs money.
3. The payment surcharge/fee model matters (hand-off to Agent 05/07 on Nayax/Cantaloupe fees; Agent 08 on surcharging rules).

## 3. Health and wellness

- **Regulation-led:** the Victorian *Healthy Choices policy directive for public health services* (Jan 2025) applies to **all vending machines and in-house retail** at public hospitals and health services:
  - Food: **≥50% GREEN, ≤20% RED**, and no RED items promoted.
  - Drinks: **no sugary RED drinks**.
  - Combination machines: ≥50% GREEN, ≤20% AMBER artificially sweetened drinks, ≤20% RED foods, and no RED drinks.
  - Products are classified with the free **FoodChecker** tool; the department also publishes plan-o-grams (shelf layout templates).
  - Sources: https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices ; https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices/healthy-vending/planograms ; https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/HS_Healthy-choices-policy-directive-food-and-drinks-targets_Aug2024.pdf
- **Adoption example:** Northern Health (Kilmore District Hospital) committed to ≥50% green food in all vending from Sep 2024. https://www.nh.org.au/2024/08/healthy-choices-in-kilmore-making-it-easier-for-staff-to-eat-well/
- Healthy Choices *guidelines* also exist for workplaces, sport and recreation centres, and schools. They are voluntary or guidance for non-health sites, but are often adopted by councils (e.g. council-run leisure centres). https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/workplaces-hospitals/healthy-choices-guidelines (verify scope; hand-off to Agent 08).
- **Demand-led (ESTIMATE, not quantified here):**
  - Protein bars and drinks, sugar-free/zero drinks, kombucha and functional drinks are growing in Australian convenience retail.
  - Gyms and office "wellness" programs want healthy ranges.
  - **Caveat:** in many blue-collar sites, full-sugar drinks, energy drinks and confectionery still dominate sales. Healthy-only machines are a **positioning choice, not a universal demand.**

## 4. Convenience and 24/7 access

- Melbourne's growth corridors and industrial precincts have thin food retail and limited late-night options. **ESTIMATE:** the core vending customer is a shift worker in a site with no on-site canteen after 3 pm.
- Food delivery apps (Uber Eats, DoorDash, Menulog) compete for meal occasions but not for impulse "now" purchases. They are also expensive (A$15+ with fees), which favours fresh-food smart fridges at A$8–12 (ESTIMATE).

## 5. Hybrid work changes office demand

- Office vacancy in the Melbourne CBD is 19% (Jan 2026, Property Council). About 36% of workers usually WFH (ABS, Aug 2024).
- Office demand is concentrated on Tuesday–Thursday. Employers are using food perks to entice staff back, which is an argument for **subsidised micro-markets** (the employer pays part of the price). Morsl, Provender and Agora all market this. **ESTIMATE:** the "employer-subsidised pantry" is a growing B2B model in Melbourne.

## 6. Product / cultural preferences (Melbourne specifics)

- **Multicultural population:** net overseas migration was about 77% of Melbourne's 2024–25 growth; the main source countries are India and China (background knowledge, verify). International students number about 322k enrolments.
  - **ESTIMATE:** Asian snacks/drinks (Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Indian), bubble tea and instant noodles sell well at universities, PBSA and inner-city sites.
  - Novelty "Japanese-style" machines are a visible niche (anecdotal; searches ran out before this could be quantified).
- **Coffee culture:** Melbourne has a famously strong café culture. **ESTIMATE:** automated coffee machines compete poorly with baristas in inner suburbs, but do well in 24/7 industrial and hospital night-shift settings.
- **Energy drinks:** high demand among blue-collar and shift workers (ESTIMATE). Note that energy-drink sales to under-18s are not legally banned in Victoria as of the research date (verify with Agent 08). Some school or health-service policies exclude them.

## 7. Sustainability and ethics

- **Container Deposit Scheme:** Victoria's **CDS Vic** launched 1 Nov 2023 with a 10c refund on eligible drink containers. It is relevant to pricing and sourcing. Hand-off to Agent 04/08 (from background knowledge; not re-verified in this session).
- **ESTIMATE:** corporate and university clients increasingly ask about energy-efficient machines (LED lighting, natural refrigerants), recycling and local products. This matters at tender time.

## 8. Key uncertainties

- There is no Australian survey of vending-specific consumer behaviour. All vending-specific numbers here are operator, vendor or modelled ESTIMATES.
- The cash share is rising slightly, which is unusual globally. Watch whether it continues.
