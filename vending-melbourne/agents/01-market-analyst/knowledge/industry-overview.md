## 1. Jargon, explained once

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vending machine (spiral/coil)** | Classic glass-front machine. Products sit in spirals that turn and drop the item. Snack, drink or combo. |
| **Smart fridge / smart cooler** | Locked glass-door fridge. You tap a card to unlock it, take items, and are charged by weight sensors, RFID tags or AI cameras. |
| **Micro-market** | Self-service "mini convenience store" inside a workplace: open shelves, fridges and a self-checkout kiosk. Needs a secure, closed site (staff only). |
| **Unattended retail** | Umbrella term covering vending, smart fridges, micro-markets and kiosks. |
| **Operator** | The business that owns and services the machines. That is what the founder would be. |
| **Route** | The set of machines/locations an operator services on a regular schedule. Routes are often bought and sold as businesses. |
| **Commission** | The share of sales (or a flat fee) paid to the site host. |
| **Cashless** | Card, phone-wallet or app payments, via a telemetry card reader (e.g. Nayax). |
| **Telemetry** | The remote link a machine uses to report sales, stock levels and faults. |

### Triangulated ESTIMATE

**ESTIMATE: Australian vending/unattended-retail gross sales are probably A$1–3bn a year**, with Victoria at roughly 25% (A$250–750m).

*Reasoning:* There are perhaps 50,000–100,000 machines nationally (see §3), averaging A$8–9k gross a year each (≈A$700/month, see §5), which gives A$0.4–0.9bn for classic machines. Add in-house corporate fleets, Coca-Cola/Frucor/Asahi drink-company machines, smart fridges and micro-markets, and the total plausibly reaches A$1–3bn. Ken Research's A$5bn+ probably counts a wider definition (e.g. ticketing, laundry or office coffee). The Victorian share is based on population: Victoria ≈ 26% of Australia.

**Takeaway for the founder:** the market is mature and growing slowly (about 3–4% a year), in line with population growth plus inflation. Growth sits in **formats** (cashless, smart fridges, micro-markets, healthy ranges) rather than in total volume.

## 3. Industry structure and number of machines

- **Associations:**
  - The *Australian Vending Buying Association (AVBA)* got ACCC clearance (a collective-bargaining notification) to negotiate jointly with suppliers. The ACCC decided not to object on 8 Jul 2021, and protection runs **until 23 Jun 2027**. https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/media/media-updates/australian-vending-buying-association-to-collectively-bargain-with-suppliers (via search summary)
  - The *Independent Vending Machine Operators Association (IVMOA)* lodged a similar notification on 30 Jan 2020, and the ACCC did not object (7 Apr 2020). https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/public-registers/documents/CB10000473%20-%20IVMOA%20-%20ACCC%20to%20Interested%20Parties%20re%20Consultation%20Process%20-%2018.02.20%20-%20PR.pdf
  - **Relevance:** a small new operator may be able to join a buying group and get better stock prices. Hand-off to Agent 06.
- **Machine counts:** a secondary blog reports that the AVBA told the ACCC in 2021 its members ran about **6,000** vending, advertising, gift and PPE machines, and that a rival association had about **12,000** more. https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/start-vending-machine-business-australia (not verified against the ACCC primary document).
  - **ESTIMATE:** total Australian machines in service is 50,000–100,000. This includes drink-company-owned machines (Coca-Cola Europacific Partners, Asahi/Schweppes), corporate in-house fleets and the many sole traders who do not belong to any association. Low confidence.
- **Market shape:** the market is fragmented. A few large national/state operators sit alongside many sole-trader "side hustle" operators. Benleigh Vending has described itself as the largest independent operator in Australia (https://www.nayax.com/hu/news/benleigh-vending-au-chooses-nayax-prepaid-solution-to-fight-covid-19/). Competitor detail goes to Agent 02.
- **Beverage majors** place and service their own branded coolers and machines. Coca-Cola launched AI-powered **Coke&Go smart coolers at Sydney Airport T2 in Dec 2024** (IMARC summary). These brands compete with independents for premium sites.

## 4. Format trends

| Trend | Evidence | Implication for a new Melbourne operator |
|---|---|---|
| **Cashless is now the norm** | Benleigh reported about 60% of sales cashless across its fleet, and 85% at sites with younger users (Nayax case study, c.2020). The RBA 2025 Consumer Payments Survey finds cash at only ~15% of payments by number (see consumer-trends.md). | Every new machine needs a card/phone-wallet reader. Budget for the hardware and per-transaction fees (Agent 05). Keep cash optional for older/blue-collar sites. |
| **Smart fridges / AI coolers** | Coke&Go at Sydney Airport (Dec 2024). IMARC and Expert Market Research both flag "smart technologies" as the main growth driver. Global smart vending was USD 5.91bn in 2021 with a forecast 18% CAGR (a global figure only). | Smart fridges carry fresh food (meals, salads), which raises spend per transaction, but they need a secure, low-theft site. Lower capital cost than a spiral machine is possible (Agent 05 to verify). |
| **Micro-markets** | Morsl calls itself Australia's first unattended micro-market operator and reported 342% revenue growth in FY22 with a $5m Series A (https://yespress.io/morsl). Provender franchises micro-markets, vending and office coffee, has operated since 2008, and has a Dandenong franchise listed for sale in 2025 (https://www.commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/21052-premium-micro-market-unattended-retail-franchise-business-dandenong-2020004398). Agora Markets targets Melbourne workplaces. | Strong fit for 100+ staff workplaces now being redesigned for hybrid work. Established players exist. The franchise route (Provender) is one of the brief's options (hand-off to Agent 00/07). |
| **Healthy vending** | Victorian public health services must meet the **Healthy Choices policy directive (Jan 2025)**: at least 50% GREEN, at most 20% RED foods, and no sugary RED drinks (https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices). | Hospitals are a large captive market, but compliance is mandatory. A ready-made "healthy range" is a competitive edge (Agent 04/08). |
| **Niche / specialty vending** | Seen in practice: PPE (Benleigh, COVID era), phone accessories, gifts and Asian/Japanese snack novelty machines. Not quantified. | Niches have higher margins but small and uncertain demand. Validate before buying machines. |
| **Telemetry and data** | Nayax global ARPU (average revenue per unit) was USD 215 TTM (12 months to Dec 2024), up 12%. Total transaction value was USD 4.9bn, up 36% (global). https://quantisnow.com/insight/nayax-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results-5922026 | Telemetry lets an operator restock based on data rather than fixed visits, which cuts route cost (Agent 06). |

## 6. Headwinds and risks (market-side)

1. **Hybrid work.** Melbourne CBD office vacancy is **19% (Jan 2026)**, the highest of any capital-city CBD and up from 17.9% six months earlier (Property Council: https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/melbournes-cbd-vacancy-continues-to-be-worst-in-the-nation). It was 18% in Jan 2025, its highest level since 1997 (Fitzroys Q1 2025). Roy Morgan (Sep 2025) reports that Melbourne CBD worker numbers are struggling to return to pre-pandemic levels. **CBD office vending is a weaker segment.**
2. **Low barriers to entry.** Social-media "passive income" content drives an oversupply of sole traders, which bids up site commissions and pushes down prices. Second-hand routes are common on broker sites (e.g. Link Business, a 60-machine Brisbane route: https://linkbusiness.com.au/BR01993).
3. **Competition from the drinks majors and convenience channels** (7-Eleven, food delivery and on-site cafés).
4. **Healthy-food regulation** in public health services, and possibly in other government sites, narrows the range of high-margin confectionery and sugary drinks.
5. **Cost inflation** in stock, fuel and card-payment fees (Agents 04/06).
