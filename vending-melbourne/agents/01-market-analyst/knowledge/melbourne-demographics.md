# Melbourne Demographics Relevant to Vending

*Agent 01 Market Analyst · Research date: 4 Nov 2026 · Sources accessed 4 Nov 2026 via web search summaries (direct page fetches were blocked in this session).*

Vending depends on **concentrations of people who are stuck somewhere, hungry, and outside normal retail hours** (captive audiences). This file maps where those people are in Greater Melbourne.

## 2. Industrial and logistics workforce (blue-collar, shift-based)

Melbourne's industrial land is organised into **State-Significant Industrial Precincts (SSIPs)** under the *Melbourne Industrial and Commercial Land Use Plan (MICLUP, 2020)*, which gained stronger planning effect via amendment VC215 (Mar 2023).
- More than two-thirds of vacant zoned industrial land (~4,190 of ~6,560 ha) is in the five SSIPs. https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/strategies-and-initiatives/melbourne-industrial-and-commercial-land-use-plan
- SSIPs (from the MICLUP plan; the list is from background knowledge, so verify):
  - **Western** (Laverton North, Derrimut, Truganina): the main logistics/distribution hub.
  - **Northern** (Campbellfield, Somerton, Epping).
  - **Southern** (Dandenong South), named explicitly in the Southern Region framework: https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0026/637910/southern_chapter04_productivity.pdf
  - **Port of Melbourne** precinct.
  - **Hastings / Port of Hastings**.
- **Melbourne Airport** (Tullamarine) also anchors a large employment and freight precinct.
- No per-precinct job counts could be retrieved. **ESTIMATE:** together, the western, northern and south-eastern industrial belts employ several hundred thousand people. Manufacturing, transport/postal/warehousing and wholesale trade together are roughly 15–18% of Victoria's employment (from background knowledge of ABS Labour Force industry splits; verify).

**Why it matters:** warehouses, factories and distribution centres:
- run 24/7 or two to three shifts;
- often have no café within walking distance;
- have 50–500+ staff on site;
- have predictable demand (drinks, energy drinks, hearty snacks, meals).

This is the classic, durable vending segment.

## 3. National Employment and Innovation Clusters (NEICs)

Plan Melbourne identifies **seven NEICs** (knowledge-based job hubs): Monash, Parkville, Fishermans Bend, Dandenong, La Trobe, Sunshine and Werribee. https://vpa.vic.gov.au/national-employment-clusters/
- **Monash NEIC:** about **75,000 jobs**, the largest. It includes Monash University Clayton, Monash Medical Centre, the CSIRO and manufacturing.
- **Sunshine NEIC:** about **14,600 jobs**, a significant western hub.
- **Dandenong NEIC:** includes the Dandenong Metropolitan Activity Centre and part of the Southern Industrial Precinct.
- **Parkville:** the main hospital and university precinct (Royal Melbourne, Royal Children's, Royal Women's, Peter Mac and the University of Melbourne). No job count was retrieved.

(Planning documents: https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/628416/plan-melbourne-outcome-1.pdf. The job figures are from search summaries of Plan Melbourne material and may be dated, around 2016–2017.)

**Implication:**
- Students have high cashless adoption and a strong taste for Asian snacks/drinks and energy drinks.
- Demand is semester-driven, with a summer drop.
- Universities usually contract out vending centrally (often to a single large operator), so they are hard for a newcomer to enter.
- **Student accommodation buildings** (purpose-built student accommodation, PBSA) are more accessible.
- **PBSA sizing (added 4 Nov 2026, Phase 2):**
  - Melbourne holds ~42% of Australia's PBSA beds and is the largest PBSA market (Cushman & Wakefield, Jul 2025). https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/australia/news/2025/07/melbourne-australia-purpose-built-student-accommodation-capital
  - CBRE (2026) expects ~34k new beds nationally over 2026–29, ≈ a one-third uplift on current stock, with Melbourne taking 28%. It estimates ~10k beds of unmet demand in Melbourne City/Inner North. https://www.cbre.com.au/insights/reports/australian-student-accommodation-2026-pbsa-demand-supply-and-pricing
  - **ESTIMATE:** a national stock of ~100k beds implies ~40–45k PBSA beds in Melbourne.
- **International student cap:**
  - The 2026 National Planning Level is 295k, up from 270k in 2025. https://ieaa.org.au/IEAA/News/2025/Australian-Government-2026-National-Planning-Level.aspx
  - New commencements were down ~15% y/y to Dec 2025, while total enrolments fell ~3% (academicjobs.com, lower quality). https://www.academicjobs.com/au/higher-education-news/decline-in-international-student-commencements-australia-10167
  - Read this as flat-to-soft demand.

## 6. Apartment density / residential

- Inner Melbourne (CBD, Southbank, Docklands, Carlton, North Melbourne) has some of Australia's tallest residential towers. Southbank had about 18,700 residents in an older CBRE-cited figure, with a forecast of 50,000+ by 2036. Docklands is planned for about 20,000 residents and 60,000 workers. Neither figure is current.
- Example towers: Premiere Tower (660 apartments, 78 floors), Southbank Place (593 apartments), Melbourne Square (227 residences).
- Apartment launches hit a low of about 2,600 in 2023 (Charter Keck Cramer), with about 20,000 approved but not started. (Sources: urban.com.au, theurbandeveloper.com, as summarised in search, accessed 4 Nov 2026.)

**ESTIMATE:** large apartment towers (300+ units) with a lobby, gym or mail room can each support one or two machines (snacks, drinks, essentials such as phone chargers or toiletries). They are an under-used segment in Australia compared with Asia. Building managers and owners' corporations are the decision-makers (Agents 03/08).

## 7. Office workers (CBD)

- **Melbourne CBD office vacancy: 19% (Jan 2026)**, the worst of any capital-city CBD (Property Council: https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/melbournes-cbd-vacancy-continues-to-be-worst-in-the-nation).
  - **Update (verified 4 Nov 2026):** the Property Council reports **18.9% (Jul 2026)**, edging down from 19.0%. It is still the highest of any capital-city CBD. JLL puts it at 19.7% (Q1 2026, different method). https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/melbourne-office-market-shows-signs-of-recovery-but-nations-highest-vacancy-underscores-work-ahead ; https://www.jll.com/en-au/insights/are-structural-issues-distorting-melbourne-s-vacancy
- About 36% of employed Australians usually work from home (ABS Working Arrangements, Aug 2024: https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/earnings-and-working-conditions/working-arrangements).
- Roy Morgan (Sep 2025): Melbourne CBD worker numbers lag pre-pandemic levels. https://roymorgan-cms-prod.s3.ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/30051748/10037-Sydney-and-Brisbane-CBD-workers-surge-while-Melbourne-struggles-to-regain-pre-pandemic-heights-September-2025.pdf

**Implication:** office sites have Tuesday–Thursday peaks and weak Mondays and Fridays. CBD offices are surrounded by cafés, so classic vending competes poorly there. Micro-markets or smart fridges marketed as a "staff perk" (employer-subsidised) fit better.

## 8. Shift workers

- The latest ABS-specific figure found: about **16% of employees (1.4m) usually worked shift work**, and about 15% of those worked regular night or evening shifts (ABS 6342.0, Nov 2009: https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/products/C3AEAD8F78CE3024CA257B5F0021DC4F). **Stale.** The current ABS Working Arrangements (Aug 2024) release could not be read.
- **ESTIMATE:** about 15% of Greater Melbourne's roughly 3m workers do shift work, which is about 450,000 people. They are concentrated in healthcare, logistics, manufacturing, hospitality, security and transport. These are the core 24/7 vending customers.
