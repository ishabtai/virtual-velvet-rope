### Rationale and evidence

**A: Classic DIY route (4)**
- **Strengths:**
  - Industrial/logistics shift sites (S1, segment score 22/25) are captive, 24/7 and accessible to a newcomer. The Dandenong NEIC alone has 66,300+ jobs (03 `location-types.md`).
  - Melbourne grew by +105k people in 2024–25, concentrated in the west, north and south-east growth corridors where the distribution centres are (`melbourne-demographics.md`).
- **Limits:**
  - Market growth is only ~3–4% CAGR (IMARC, Ken Research and Expert Market Research summaries in `industry-overview.md` §2), so a new operator takes sites from incumbents.
  - "Free full service" is universal (02 finding 1), which means competition on service and range, not price.
  - The west/north industrial belt is the most contested area (02 finding 7).
- **Why not 5:** A captures none of the format growth unless fridges are added.

**B: Smart fridge / micro-market (4 / 3)**
- **Why fridges score 4:**
  - Growth is in formats, not volume: Morsl raised a $5m Series A, and Nouri and Machines Vending offer $0-hire micro-markets (02).
  - Baskets are bigger: A$8–12 ESTIMATE, against A$4–5 at a classic machine.
  - Employer-subsidy models reduce revenue risk.
- **Why full micro-markets score 3:**
  - The minimum viable site is 80–150 staff (00, citing US/UK guides).
  - Large CBD and corporate sites are tendered to Compass, Morsl and Nouri (02).
  - Melbourne CBD office vacancy is still **18.9% (Jul 2026)**, the highest of any capital-city CBD (Property Council, verified this round), and ~36% of workers usually work from home (ABS Aug 2025, via SBS).
- **New this round:** CCEP is rolling out "several hundred" Coke&Go AI coolers across AU/NZ over 2025–26 (foodanddrinkbusiness.com.au; CCEP NZ release). No Australian workplace deployments were found, but drink-led coolers will compete at high-traffic sites.
- **Where the room is:** fresh-food fridges at 30–150-staff suburban workplaces and 100–300-staff warehouses (02 `smart-fridge-micromarket-players.md`).

**C: Niche / specialty (2)**
- No Australian demand data exists (my open question #11 is still unresolved).
- Pizza vending history shows the format is fragile (00).
- There is a reputational and regulatory risk around gacha machines (Vending Times, Jun 2026, via 00).
- The plausible niches are **Asian snacks and noodles in PBSA and campus-adjacent sites** and **gym protein lines**. Both can sit inside A as one or two machines (04 finding 7).

**D: Buy existing route (3)**
- **For:** past sales prove demand at the route's sites, and this is the only archetype that starts at scale.
- **Against:**
  - The segment mix is inherited, often scattered, office-heavy or legacy.
  - Genuine routes are rare: most "for sale" listings are placement packages (02 finding 4).
  - The market is mature, so a bought route grows only if it is upgraded (cashless, fridges, better sites).

**E: Franchise / licence (3)**
- **For:** Provender and Ausbox give brand credibility with corporate, government and warehouse hosts (00, 02). That opens S2.
- **Against:** territory and supplier constraints stop the founder from concentrating on the best industrial cluster. The demand case is otherwise the same as A or B.

**F: Placement package (2)**
- The sites are picked by the seller, not by the segment matrix.
- Some sites are "being finalised" (03 finding 7), and re-site guarantees run only 180 days (09).
- Market-wise, the founder buys a random mix of segments at a premium.

**G: Placement-only (1)**
- Host commission norms are 0–10% (02, 03), and operators already prospect for themselves.
- ESTIMATE: there is no visible Melbourne market for paid locator services.

## 2. Top 3 findings for the final options

1. **The segment matters more than the archetype.**
   - Every viable option should anchor on **industrial/logistics shift sites in ONE cluster**: South-east (Dandenong South/Keysborough/Braeside) or West (Laverton North/Truganina/Derrimut).
   - The market grows only ~3–4% a year, so returns come from site selection and route density, not market tailwind.
   - Archetypes that don't let the founder choose segments (F, G, and to a degree D and E) score lower on demand.
2. **Format growth is real, but the gap is mid-size sites, not big micro-markets.**
   - Large corporate and CBD sites are tendered or contested by Morsl, Nouri, Compass and Coke&Go, which is rolling out several hundred AI coolers in AU/NZ across 2025–26.
   - CBD offices remain weak: vacancy is 18.9% in Jul 2026, only marginally improved from 19.0%.
   - The opening is **smart fridges at 30–300-staff suburban and industrial sites**. This argues for "A + B-lite" as the strongest market-fit option.
3. **Secondary clusters are useful as fillers, not as anchors. Novelty niches are not a strategy.**
   - PBSA (student accommodation) is sizeable:
     - Melbourne holds ~42% of Australia's PBSA beds (Cushman & Wakefield, Jul 2025).
     - ESTIMATE: that is ~40–45k beds, on a national base of ~100k inferred from CBRE's "34k beds ≈ one-third uplift".
     - CBRE estimates ~10k beds of unmet demand in Melbourne City/Inner North.
   - The international student cap is rising (295k for 2026, up from 270k), but new commencements fell ~15% y/y to Dec 2025, so demand is flat to soft. Expect seasonal dips in Dec–Feb.
   - Gyms, PBSA and dealerships are cluster fillers. Niche concepts should be tested inside a classic route.

## 4. Conflicts with other agents' knowledge bases

1. **Industrial-site revenue.**
   - 03 gives A$250–800/wk for warehouses, 07 uses a A$175/wk base, and my earlier figure was A$800–2,500/month (A$185–575/wk).
   - Recommendation: 07 should model industrial sites as a "High" band (A$300/wk) rather than at 03's top end until telemetry exists.
2. **Archetype labels.** 09's INDEX calls micro-market "C" and niche "D", but in 00's scheme they are B and C. 00 should normalise the labels before synthesis.
3. **Smart-fridge site size.**
   - 02 sees **20–100-staff** sites as the under-served gap, while 00 quotes an 80–150-staff minimum for micro-markets.
   - Both hold: one fridge can work at ~30–80 staff, while a full micro-market needs 120+.
   - I treat sites under ~30 staff as uneconomic.
4. **Host commission.**
   - 04's margin example uses a 15% commission, while 02, 03 and 07 use ~10%.
   - Industrial sites are more often 0–10%, so 15% is conservative. Use 10% as the base.
5. **Healthy Choices dates.**
   - My knowledge base says "directive (Jan 2025)".
   - 03 and 04 show the drinks rules became mandatory on **30 Sep 2022**, and the directive was then updated in Jan 2025.
   - These are consistent, so I have reworded nothing material. Note for 08.
6. **Cashless.** 07's 80% and my 75–90% are consistent. 05's "~4% card cost" assumes a high cashless share, so the two fit together.
