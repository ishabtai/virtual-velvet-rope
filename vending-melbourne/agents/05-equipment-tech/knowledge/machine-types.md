## Jargon (explained once)
- **Spiral / coil machine** — products sit in metal helix coils; the coil turns to drop the item. The classic snack machine.
- **Combo** — one cabinet with snacks on spirals above and chilled drinks below (or a single refrigerated cabinet holding both).
- **Glass-front drinks (GFV)** — drinks machine with a window and an elevator/"lift" that collects the bottle (gentler than a drop).
- **Stack / can vendor** — older closed-front drinks machine with columns of cans (cheap, durable, limited range).
- **MDB** — Multi-Drop Bus, the industry-standard internal protocol that lets a coin mech, note reader, or card reader talk to the machine's controller. If a machine is "MDB-compliant", almost any modern card reader (Nayax, Vendcell etc.) can be plugged in. **Never buy a machine that isn't MDB (or has no supported adaptor).**
- **Smart fridge / smart cooler** — a locked glass-door fridge; customer taps a card to unlock, takes items, and the fridge charges for what was removed using AI cameras (computer vision), RFID tags, or weight sensors.
- **Micro-market** — open shelves + fridges + a self-checkout kiosk inside a secure workplace (unstaffed mini convenience store).
- **Telemetry** — the machine reports sales, stock and faults over the mobile network (4G) to the operator's software.

## Power usage (running cost input for Agent 07)
- Refrigerated vending typically uses **2,500–4,000 kWh/yr**; a beverage machine ≈ 3,500 kWh/yr (~0.4 kWh/h) [15][16]. Energy Star-class units ~1,450 kWh/yr [17].
- Ambient snack machines: lighting + controller only — ESTIMATE 300–700 kWh/yr.
- Victorian small-business electricity: the 2026-27 Victorian Default Offer fell ~6% for small business from 1 Jul 2026 [18]. ESTIMATE usage rate ~30–38 c/kWh incl. GST on market offers.
- **ESTIMATE annual electricity per machine:** chilled combo/drinks ≈ 2,500–3,500 kWh × $0.33 ≈ **$825–$1,150/yr**; Energy-efficient/LED/new unit ≈ $500–$700/yr; snack ≈ $100–$230/yr.
- In practice the **site host usually pays electricity** (it's the main "commission" in a free-placement deal) — this is a negotiation point for Agent 03.
- Energy-saving: LED retrofits and occupancy-sensing "misers" (~US$165, 1–2 yr payback) [16].

## Security & durability
- Indoor, staffed workplaces: standard machines fine.
- Semi-public (sports clubs, apartment foyers, train-adjacent): glass-front prone to vandalism; prefer steel-front or place within CCTV; bolt/anchor to wall or floor (also a tip-over safety issue).
- Fully public/outdoor: requires weatherised cabinet, anti-pry doors, high-security T-handle locks, no cash (cashless-only reduces break-in motive) — ESTIMATE +$1,000–$3,000 per unit.
- Cashless-only machines remove the cash-collection and theft risk; Australian vending is now heavily card/phone-wallet based (see payments-and-telemetry.md).

## Maintenance & warranty
- New machines from Australian suppliers typically come with 12-month warranty (ESTIMATE — confirm per quote). Used: 0–3 months unless refurbished by a dealer.
- Common failures: spiral motors, refrigeration compressors/fans, note acceptors (if cash), card reader connectivity.
- Choose brands with **parts available in Australia** — sources note well-known brands are easier to repair because parts are stocked locally [3].
- Smart fridges have fewer moving parts (no spirals) but depend on camera/software accuracy (vendor claims "99% recognition") [11] and on the supplier's cloud — supplier lock-in risk.
- Budget **ESTIMATE $300–$600/machine/yr** for repairs/parts on used equipment; less on new under warranty.

| Scenario | Machine choice |

| Mid budget ($20–50k), workplace focus | 2–4 new combos or AI smart fridges; telemetry from day 1 |
| Higher budget ($50k+), scalable | Smart fridges + 1 micro-market pilot at a 150+ staff site |
