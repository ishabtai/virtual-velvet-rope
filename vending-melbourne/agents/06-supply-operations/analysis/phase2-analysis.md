*Date: 4 Nov 2026 · AUD (ex GST unless stated) · Lens: **operational feasibility for a beginner**. Can a first-time operator source the stock, keep machines full and legal, and hold the workload within the hours available, without the cost and complexity of operations eating the margin?*

- My knowledge base (`knowledge/*.md`).

## 2. Operating plans for the shortlisted options

All figures are **ESTIMATE** unless cited. They are derived from `knowledge/route-planning.md` §4 (25 min per stop including access, about 8 min pre-kitting per machine, about 35 km/h urban average, ATO 88c/km as an all-in vehicle cost). Stops are assumed **3–5 km apart** inside one industrial cluster, with the storage base about 15 km away. Prospecting time to win sites (03: 100 walk-ins → 3–6 placements) is **not** included here. Add about 3–5 h/week during build-out.

### 2.1 A: Classic DIY route (Dandenong South/Braeside or Laverton North/Truganina)

**Sourcing**
- Costco business membership (about $65/yr, most likely figure; sources still conflict). Moorabbin and Ardeer are the nearest warehouses to the two clusters.
- Campbells cash & carry: free for ABN holders, confirmed on its FAQ. Clayton is a confirmed VIC store. ScrapeHero counted 4 VIC stores in Dec 2024.
- Coles/Woolworths half-price specials for drinks while under 10 machines.
- At 15–20 machines, ask CCEP/Asahi for delivered drinks (WA contract minimum: 10 cartons).

**Visit frequency**
- Weekly at an average site.
- 2×/week at the top ~20% of sites: 24/7 shift sites selling more than $300/wk.
- With telemetry, skip a visit when no column is more than 50% empty.

| Item | 5 machines | 20 machines |
|---|---|---|
| On-site (25 min/stop) | 2.1 h | 8.3 h + 1.3 h (4 second visits) |
| Driving (stops + base return) | ~50 km → 1.4 h | ~120 km → 3.4 h |
| Pre-kitting at base | 0.7 h | 2.7 h |
| Wholesale runs | 0.75 h (fortnightly) | 2.5 h (weekly, partly delivered) |
| Admin, telemetry, refunds, banking | 0.5 h | 1.5 h |
| **Total ops hours/week** | **~5.5 h (≈1.1 h/machine)** | **~20 h (≈1.0 h/machine)** |
| Vehicle km/week (incl. wholesale) | ~70 km → **~$62/wk** | ~190 km → **~$167/wk** |
| Vehicle | Own wagon/SUV. About 75 kg of stock a week (≈240 units) | Used van (2017 HiAce LWB listed at $24,950). About 300 kg a week |
| Storage | Home garage. 2–3 weeks of stock ≈ $1.2k at cost, ~25 cartons | ~$4.8k at cost, 60–80 cartons ≈ 6–9 m³. Garage, or a **food-permitted** unit (see §4) |
| Spoilage / shrink | 1–3% of COGS | 1–3% of COGS (2% base) |

Scattered sanity check: the same 20 machines spread across Melbourne at about 15 km between stops is about 300+ km and about 30 h/week. Density is worth roughly 10 h/week.

### 2.2 A+C: Classic + Asian snack/noodle line

- **Sourcing.** As A, plus an Asian importer/wholesaler.
  - Melbourne options: **Wang Distribution and Chen Foods (both Clayton South)**, **Golden Sun Trading (Dandenong)**, New Eastland, and Asian Pantry's wholesale/bulk section. Sources are third-party directory listings; trade terms and minimums were not found.
  - All of these are inside or beside the SE cluster, so this is an **operational argument for Dandenong South over the West**.
  - Rules (04/08): buy only from Australian importers with English labels; drinks must be CDS-registered.
- **Visits.** Same as A. The niche machine sits on the normal weekly loop and adds **0 extra trips**.
- **Wholesale time.** About +0.5 h per fortnight.
- **Hours.** 5 machines (4 combo + 1 Asian) ≈ **6 h/wk**. 20 machines (16 + 4) ≈ **21 h/wk**.
- **Vehicle and storage.** As A, plus about 10–15 cartons of noodles and snacks. Cup noodles are bulky but light.
- **Spoilage.** **2–4%** on the niche machine. Velocity is untested and some imported snacks have 6–9-month best-befores, so pull at 14 days. The rest of the route stays at 1–3%.
- **Hot water for cup noodles.** The host must supply it. Agree this at placement. A noodle machine without nearby hot water is a planogram mistake.

### 2.3 A+B-lite: Combos + small smart fridges (30–300 staff, one cluster)

**Phasing (strongly recommended)**
- **Stage 1 (months 1–3 of each fridge):** long-life chilled only. That means Up&Go, yoghurt/protein pouches, flavoured milk, cheese and crackers, and drinks (04's mix). These keep 2–6 weeks. They remain Class 4 or near it, can be bought at Costco/Campbells, and can be visited weekly or twice-weekly.
- **Stage 2 (fresh food):** only after 4 weeks of sales data.
  - Sandwiches and wraps (3–5-day life) mean **Class 3 FoodTrader registration** with council fees (e.g. Glen Eira $194 for 2025–26; check Greater Dandenong or Wyndham), cold-chain records, and 2–3 visits per fridge per week.
  - **No Melbourne pre-packaged sandwich supplier that serves vending was found.** Leads are Bidfood Port Melbourne, PFD Knoxfield, local makers (one sandwich manufacturer was listed for sale), and small bakery wholesalers with minimums of about $70 per order.
  - At about 15–30 sandwiches per fridge per week, the founder is below most broadliner sweet spots.

**Cold chain**
- Insulated crates with ice bricks for runs under 30 min. An esky or a portable 12V fridge is about $300–800 (ESTIMATE). A refrigerated van is not needed below about 5 fridges.
- Use a probe thermometer and log temperatures at pickup, at the machine and on display.
- The fridge must have telemetry temperature alarms and auto-lock above 5 °C (confirm with 05).

| Item | 5 units (4 combos + 1 fridge) | 20 units (15 combos + 5 fridges) |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge visits/week | 1–2 (long-life) / 2–3 (fresh) | 5–10 / 10–15 |
| **Total ops hours/week** | **~6.5–8 h** | **~24–28 h** |
| Vehicle km/week | ~90–120 km → $80–105 | ~250–300 km → $220–265 |
| Vehicle | Wagon + 2 eskies | Van + crates/12V fridge. A refrigerated van (new HiAce $57–69k drive-away, before refrigeration fit-out) is only worth it at 10+ fridges |
| Storage | Garage + a dedicated food-grade home fridge for buffer stock | As A, plus a commercial upright fridge at base (≈$1.5–3k) |
| Spoilage / shrink (fridge) | Long-life: **3–5%**. Fresh: **8–15%** (mis-detection adds 1–2%) | Same |

**Operational site floor.** A fridge visit costs about $25–30 (30 min at about $35/h plus vehicle). At 2 visits/week and about 45% gross margin, the fridge needs **more than about $130/wk ex GST** (≈15 baskets) just to cover visits. In practice **40+ staff on site** is the floor, consistent with 03's 40–150 sweet spot.

### 2.4 A→D: Learn, then buy

- **Phase 1 (months 0–9).** Run plan 2.1 at 3–5 machines (about 4–6 h/wk). Build the price book, pre-kit SOPs, refund log, key register, and a KPI sheet (sales/machine/week, GP per visit).
- **Phase 2 (purchase).** Buy a 10–15-machine route **inside or next to the same cluster**.
- **Integration load.**
  - Weeks 1–8 run at **+30–50% hours** (≈18–25 h/wk for about 18 machines) while learning planograms, retrofitting non-cashless machines ($1,750–2,800 each, 05), re-keying and meeting every host.
  - After that, about 1 h per machine per week, as in plan A.
- **Operational due diligence before signing:**
  1. Do 2+ ride-alongs and time each stop.
  2. Get 12–24 months of telemetry.
  3. Map every site. Reject any site more than 25 min from the cluster, or price it as relocate-or-drop.
  4. Do a stock age audit (expired stock is a write-off on day one).
  5. Check the machines are MDB and 4G.
  6. Find out which supplier accounts transfer.

### 2.5 D: Buy a route outright

- **Workload.** Full workload from day one. For a 20-machine route: **~22–30 h/wk for the first 2 months**, then 20 h/wk if clustered and 30+ h/wk if scattered.
- **Vehicle.** A van is often included; check its age.
- **Sourcing.** Inherit the seller's channels, ideally with bottler delivery already set up.
- **Spoilage.** Inherited stock at handover: budget a **one-off write-off of 5–10% of the opening stock**. Run-rate after that is 1–3%.
- **Main operational risk.** The beginner learns everything at full volume. With no backup person, a single sick week causes stock-outs and complaints, which feed site loss (03 estimates 10–20% in year 1).

### 2.6 Others (brief)

- **E.** Hours are similar to A or B-lite. The franchisor may mandate supply, which removes the Costco/specials savings. Ask for the supply terms in the disclosure document (08).
- **F.** For 5 scattered package machines, expect about **10–12 h/wk** and about 250 km/wk (≈$220/wk vehicle). That is roughly double plan A's cost per machine.
- **G.** Fewer than 2 h/wk of admin.
- **C standalone and full B.** Not recommended as a first operation.

## 4. Conflicts with other agents

1. **Cluster choice (02 vs 01/03).** 02 suggests avoiding the contested West/Dandenong belts and using Moorabbin–Braeside or inner-ring offices. Operationally, inner-ring offices add parking, loading-dock and sign-in time (+10–15 min a stop) and paid parking.
   - My lens favours the **South-East industrial belt (Dandenong South–Braeside–Moorabbin)**: Costco Moorabbin, Campbells Clayton and three Asian wholesalers are all within about 15–20 min.
   - Moorabbin–Braeside satisfies both 02 and me.
   - The West has Costco Ardeer, but I could not confirm a Campbells walk-in store there. The Metcash Truganina DC does not serve walk-in buyers.
2. **"1–2 h per machine per week" (00, archetype A).** That holds only for scattered routes. A clustered route runs at about 1 h. 07 should model both, because density is the main operational lever.
3. **Storage cost in my own Phase 1 handoff ($80–344/mo self-storage).** National Storage's prohibited-items list includes **perishable and "contained/tinned food"**, so ordinary self-storage may be off-limits for vending stock. 07 should not assume cheap self-storage. Use a garage, or a food-permitted unit or warehouse.
4. **Smart-fridge shrink (07: COGS 50% + 6% shrink).** That is fine for a long-life-chilled fridge. It is **low for a fresh-led fridge**, where 12% is the base case. 04's 5–15% fresh waste is consistent with my view.
5. **B-lite site size.** 00 says 100–300 staff; 02 and 03 say 20–150. On ops, below about 40 staff the visit cost (~$25–30/visit) is not covered at 2 visits/week, so use **40–150** as the operational band.
6. **Labour rate.** The $28/h vending ad matches the permanent award rate ($27.81). My earlier $34.76 is the casual rate, so both are right. 08 still needs to confirm the award.

## 5. Top findings

1. **Density is the dominant operational lever.** The same 20 machines take about 20 h/wk clustered and about 30 h/wk scattered. That is worth about 10 h and about $100/wk in vehicle cost. This structurally penalises F, scattered D routes, and franchisor-assigned sites.
2. **Ambient first, fresh food later.** Classic and Asian-snack lines run on Class 4 notification with 1–4% spoilage, a car and a garage. Fresh food in fridges brings Class 3 registration, ≤5 °C logs, 2–3×/week visits, 8–15% waste and **no identified small-volume Melbourne sandwich supplier**. B-lite should start with long-life chilled lines.
3. **The South-East cluster has the best supply base.** Costco Moorabbin, Campbells Clayton (free for ABN holders) and Asian wholesalers in Clayton South and Dandenong are all close, which also makes A+C cheap to run. Storage is a hidden constraint: mainstream self-storage bans food, so plan for a garage until about 10–15 machines.
