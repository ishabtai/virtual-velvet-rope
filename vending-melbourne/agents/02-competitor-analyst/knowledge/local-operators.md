---

## 1. Why local operators matter
The founder's realistic first customers (offices of 20–150 staff, gyms, warehouses, trade sites, childcare/kinder staff rooms, small aged-care facilities) are exactly what small local operators chase. These are the direct competitors, and also possible **acquisition targets** (see `routes-for-sale.md`).

### Observed pattern
- **Every** local operator offers the same free full-service model. Offers are near-indistinguishable on websites.
- Several operate as **Google-Sites pages or press-release wires** — i.e., very small, possibly 1-person businesses. `ESTIMATE`: a large share of Melbourne's operators run fewer than 30 machines.
- Commission is often "optional" or "negotiated" — small operators frequently pay **0%** to small sites and use free service as the inducement. `ESTIMATE` from VENHAR wording.
- The 62-machine example shows the scale one owner + casual staff can sustain (~$95k+/month revenue claimed, i.e. ~$1,530/machine/month, ~$18.4k/machine/year). `ESTIMATE` derived arithmetic; seller's claim not verified.

## 4. Competitive strengths and weaknesses of local operators (ESTIMATE)

| Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|
| Flexible, personal relationships with site owners | Thin websites, little proof (no reviews, no case studies) |
| Low overhead; owner-operator | Restocking cadence may lag when the owner is sick/away (single point of failure) |
| Willing to place at small sites big players ignore | Older, cash-centric machines on legacy routes; limited telemetry |
| Local knowledge of industrial precincts | Generic product mix (chips, chocolate, soft drink); weak healthy/fresh offering |

Basis: pattern of websites/listings found; general industry complaint lists (machines empty; machine takes money without vending) from https://blog.vendinggroup.com/10-complaints-about-vending-machine-companies. **No Melbourne-specific review evidence** was found — Google Maps / ProductReview.com.au review mining is an open task.

## 5. Takeaways
1. The local field is **fragmented and undifferentiated**; most compete on "free" only.
2. **Industrial west (Sunshine, Altona, Truganina, Laverton) and north (Craigieburn, Epping)** are visibly contested by SVA, VENHAR, Ausbox, Machines Vending, and the 62-machine operator.
3. **Niche/novelty vending** in Melbourne is concentrated in transport hubs and shopping centres, often short-lived or pop-up.
4. **Proof of service quality** (telemetry, response-time guarantees, public reviews) is rare — an opening.
