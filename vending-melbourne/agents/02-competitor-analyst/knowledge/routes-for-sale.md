---

## 1. Jargon
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Route / run** | A set of machines already placed at sites, sold as a going concern with the site agreements. |
| **Placement package / "new vending business"** | A seller (often the machine supplier) sells you new machines **plus** a locating service that finds sites. No trading history. *Not* an established route. |
| **Locator** | A company paid to find sites for your machines. |
| **Re-site guarantee** | Promise to move a machine to a new site if the first performs poorly (e.g. "180-day re-site option"). |
| **SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings)** | Net profit + owner's wage + one-off/personal costs. The usual basis for small-business valuation. |
| **Multiple** | Price ÷ annual SDE (or ÷ revenue). |
| **Going concern (GST)** | A sale of an operating business can be GST-free if conditions are met; placement packages usually attract GST. Hand-off to 08 Legal / 07 Finance. |

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 Price per machine
- Placement packages: **~$6,000–$11,000 per machine incl. site**, clustering around **$9,000–$10,000**. `ESTIMATE` from P1–P6.
- This is likely close to (or above) the cost of buying a new machine and finding a site yourself. Hand-off to 05 Equipment to compare new-machine cost; if a new cashless combo machine costs a similar amount, the buyer is paying a premium only for site-finding.

### 3.2 Revenue multiples (where derivable)
- **E5 Doreen:** $16,000 / $14,000 turnover ≈ **1.1× revenue**. If gross margin is ~60% (claimed by similar listings) and 10% commission applies: gross profit after commission ≈ $14,000 × (0.60 − 0.10) = **$7,000** → ≈ **2.3× pre-labour profit**. `ESTIMATE`. After fuel, card fees (~3–6% on cashless, hand-off 05) and owner time, true SDE is lower → effective multiple **~3×+**.
- **E1 62 machines:** revenue $1.14m/yr. A vending supplier blog suggests net profit after COGS, power, payment fees and restock labour is **20–30% of revenue** (weak source: https://topvending.blog/best-vending-machines-for-sale-melbourne/ via search summary). → net profit ~$230k–$340k. At **2–3× SDE** a fair price might be **~$450k–$1.0m**. `ESTIMATE`, assumption-heavy.
- **E2 $500k run:** price consistent with a route of roughly 30–60 machines at the above economics. `ESTIMATE`.

### 3.3 Typical multiples (ESTIMATE)
No Australian vending-specific multiple data was found. Working assumption for Phase 2:

| Business type | Plausible multiple | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny route (1–5 machines), owner-operated | 1.0–1.5× **revenue**, ≈2.5–4× SDE | Small-listing pricing above; buyers pay for convenience |
| Established route, 20–80 machines, contracts in writing, telemetry data | **2–3× SDE** | Common Australian small-business range for owner-operated businesses; vending has low key-person dependency but site-loss risk |
| Placement packages | Not a valuation — priced as **equipment + locating fee** | No earnings history |

Hand-off to 07 Financial Modeller to stress-test.

### 3.4 Red flags seen in listings
1. **"1 hour per week"** claims for 2–11 machines — unrealistic once driving, stock buying, cleaning and breakdowns are counted. `ESTIMATE`.
2. **"60% gross profit"** headline without net profit or turnover (P2, P4).
3. **Geographically scattered sites** (P1: Phillip Island to Ballarat) — route density is the main driver of servicing cost.
4. **Site agreements**: unclear whether sites are contracted to the buyer or the placement company; whether the host can terminate at will. Seller-side guidance flagged checking "whether the placement operator controls the locations" (SEEK search summary).
5. **AI-generated listings** — at least one Provender listing marked "AI generated. Contact seller to confirm."
6. **No independent reviews** of placement companies found; one South African review site had a "scam" complaint about an unrelated vending seller (https://www.hellopeter.com/pk-vending/reviews/pk-vending-is-a-scam-dont-invest-your-money-2296858) — illustrative of category risk only.

### 3.5 Due-diligence checklist for any route purchase
- 12–24 months of **machine-level** sales (telemetry/Nayax/Cantaloupe exports, not spreadsheets).
- Signed **site agreements**: term, notice period, commission, exclusivity, assignability to new owner.
- Host contact for each top site (with seller's permission) to confirm they'll stay.
- Machine age, model, cashless reader ownership/contract, refrigeration condition, any lease/finance on machines.
- Product supplier accounts and pricing; stock on hand at valuation.
- Route map: total km/week, parking/loading access, after-hours access.
- Revenue concentration: % of revenue from top 5 sites (if >50%, discount heavily).
- GST: going-concern treatment; council/permit or body-corporate approvals (hand-off 08).

---

## 4. Takeaways
1. **Very few genuine established Melbourne routes surface**; most "vending businesses for sale" are new placement packages sold by machine suppliers.
2. **Package pricing ≈ $9–10k per machine**, roughly 1× annual revenue for small units — expensive relative to likely profit.
3. **Mid-sized routes ($150k–$500k)** exist but disclose little; the 62-machine route (~$1.14m revenue) shows a single operator can scale with casual labour.
4. **Buying a route is a valid entry option** for a founder with ~$150k+ capital who values day-one cash flow; the biggest risk is site-agreement transferability.
