# Phase 2 Analysis: Business-Model Archetypes Through the Competitor Lens

**Agent:** 02 Competitor Analyst · **Date:** 4 Nov 2026 · **Input:** `agents/00-lead-strategist/knowledge/business-model-archetypes.md` (archetypes A–G and four hybrids)

> **What this lens measures.** Each score (1–5) answers one question: *how winnable is this model for a beginner, given who already competes for the same sites, customers or deals in Melbourne?* 5 = weak or absent incumbents, a defensible opening; 1 = saturated, or the beginner is the incumbents' product. The score does not cover profitability (07), equipment (05) or legality (08).
>
> **Evidence base.** Phase 1 knowledge files in `../knowledge/` plus 8 new web searches run on 4 Nov 2026. WebFetch is still blocked for `bsale.com.au` (DNS not allowed in this session), so listing details are still search-summary level. `ESTIMATE` = my inference.

## 1. Scores per archetype

| # | Archetype | Score | One-line verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Classic DIY route | **3** | Fragmented local field you can beat on density and service, but with no product moat |
| B | Smart fridge / micro-market | **2** (full micro-market) / **3** (B-lite fridge, 20–100 staff) | Funded players own large sites and now industrial estates too. Small sites are still open |
| C | Niche / specialty | **2** standalone / **4** as a layer on A | Few specialists, but novelty fails. It works as a differentiator inside a route |
| D | Buy an existing route | **4** (if priced right) | The only model that skips the site-acquisition contest. Supply is thin and asks are inflated |
| E | Franchise / licence | **2** | You buy a brand that every operator matches with "free", and fees are undisclosed |
| F | Placement package | **1** | Saturated. The beginner is the seller's customer, not a competitor |
| G | Placement-only / asset-light | **2** | An incumbent locator has existed since 2006, and package sellers bundle site-finding |

### A. Classic DIY route — 3
- **For:** The long tail of Melbourne operators is one-person and small (VENHAR, VendLink, Allsorts, Vendco and others, `local-operators.md`). None of them publishes service levels, telemetry restocking or host dashboards (`gaps-and-positioning.md` §2.3). Sites with 20–100 staff are fragmented and have no tender process.
- **Against:** "Free full service" is universal. Commission runs from 0% to 10% (SVA rebate of 10%, paid every 6 months; VENHAR offers "optional commission" — https://sites.google.com/view/venharvending/home, accessed 4 Nov 2026). Coke Vending takes drink-heavy, high-traffic sites. The industrial west, north and Dandenong are the most contested areas (SVA, Ausbox, Machines Vending, the 62-machine route). A new entrant's only levers are density and service, and an incumbent can copy both.
- **Implication:** Choose one precinct the big three industrial players do not cluster in (e.g. Moorabbin–Braeside, or inner-ring office suburbs such as Richmond, Cremorne and Collingwood) instead of Truganina or Laverton. `ESTIMATE`.

### B. Smart fridge / micro-market — 2 (full) / 3 (B-lite)
- **New this round:** Morsl is not only a CBD-corporate player. It reportedly serves 13 warehouse and logistics companies across NSW and VIC, including Amazon and Officeworks. It has a national partnership with Charter Hall across that landlord's industrial portfolio (https://www.charterhall.com.au/News/news-article/2022/04/26/promoting-wellbeing-in-the-industrial-sector-with-healthy-food-provider-morsl ; https://www.procurementmag.com/articles/morsl-transforms-workplace-eating-culture-amazon-and-beyond, both accessed 4 Nov 2026, dates 2022–2023). In July 2025 it ran a 24/7 hospital staff café pilot at St John of God Ballarat (https://timesnewsgroup.com.au/ballarat/news/new-24-7-cafe-options-for-hospital-staff, accessed 4 Nov 2026). That means a funded operator ($5m Series A, ~$14.4m post-money, Jan 2023 per a profile) can reach the large logistics sheds through landlord channels (https://www.forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/morsl-adds-5-million-in-series-a-raise/).
- Nouri/Allround (free installation) and Machines Vending ("$0 cost hire") offer micro-markets free in the CBD, Dandenong and Truganina (`smart-fridge-micromarket-players.md`). A beginner who has to fund $20k–$60k of kit and then place it free is up against operators with investor capital.
- **Still open:** single smart fridges for 20–100-staff offices and smaller warehouses, which no player was found marketing to. Build-to-rent and student towers have one visible player (Provender). Score 3 for B-lite in those segments only.

### C. Niche / specialty — 2 standalone, 4 as a layer
- Melbourne niche evidence is scarce and short-lived. The Southern Cross pizza machine was a temporary placement. Pizza Gio closed. Capsule Station gacha sits in a mall tenancy, and gacha faces regulatory concern (Vending Times, 16 Jun 2026; see 00's file). No Melbourne operator was found offering culturally specific ranges, sports nutrition at gyms, or local-brand curation (`gaps-and-positioning.md` §2.2). The gap is real but unproven.
- As a **standalone business**, the risks are novelty decay and site dependence (transport hubs and malls have landlord tenders and high commission). As a **layer inside a classic route** (A+C), a niche range is the cheapest available differentiator against copycat "free full service".

### D. Buy an existing route — 4 (conditional)
- **Competitive logic:** buying a route buys the scarcest asset: sites already won from incumbents. It is the only archetype that does not start with a pitch against Coke, SVA or the locals.
- **Supply is thin.** The search this round found no new genuine established Melbourne route listed in 2026. The market is still the 62-machine route (under offer since early 2025; a new detail is a Victoria Police clearance for prison sites), a $90k vending-plus-event-hire business (AnyBusiness, updated 14 Feb 2026), and unpriced "from $150k/$200k" broker teasers (https://bsale.com.au/listing/under-offer-expansive-vic-646352 ; https://ftp.anybusiness.com.au/listings/melbourne-vic-3000-leisure-entertainment-vending-services-hire-rent-food-hospitality-catering-3447771, via search summary, 4 Nov 2026).
- **Asks are inflated.** Interstate asking prices imply about **3–3.3× profit** (Sydney mystery-box vending chain at $2.2m ask on $661k profit, and $1.5m on $500k, via search summary of linkbusiness.com.au). A Sydney route of 26 machines sold on about $40k net profit, price undisclosed. No Australian achieved-price data exists. That leaves 07's "≤2–2.5× verified SDE" rule as the walk-away price.
- **Hidden pipeline (`ESTIMATE`):** SVA claims "6,000+ machines sold to 3,000+ customers" (seller marketing, via bsale search summary). Placement-package buyers on 12-month rolling site terms are a large pool of future distressed sellers. A buyer can assemble a route cheaply from them (see the A→D hybrid).

### E. Franchise / licence — 2
- The brand's value is limited because hosts get the same free service from everyone. A franchise logo mostly helps with corporate procurement, and those sites are already Compass, Morsl or Coke tender territory.
- Fees are still undisclosed. This round's search found no Provender disclosure document, royalty or territory terms. The only benchmark is US Fresh Healthy Vending, with royalties of 6–12% of gross and a US$1–5k fee per machine (SEC 10-K FY2015/16, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001526689/000164033416001762/Financial_Report.xlsx). A 6–12% royalty on top of a 10% host commission would squeeze an already thin margin. `ESTIMATE`.
- Ausbox (Melbourne since 2004) both operates and sells licences in the same western suburbs (Altona, Truganina, Craigieburn). A licensee may compete with the licensor's own network. Ask 08 to check this.

### F. Placement package — 1
- Pricing is ~$6k–$11k per machine including site (`routes-for-sale.md` §2.2). 05 puts a new combo at ~$10.5k placed and a used combo at ~$5.5k. A buyer therefore pays new-machine money for a site that comes on a **12-month tenure that rolls over yearly** (SVA/JBS listing text via search summary, 4 Nov 2026). Some listings say sites are "being finalised", so they are not yet secured.
- Sites are scattered (P1 runs from Cowes to Ballarat). Thousands of package machines compete for the same small sites (kinders, gyms, aged care). No independent reviews or ACCC actions were found for SVA or JBS, so absence of complaints is not evidence of quality.

### G. Placement-only / asset-light — 2
- Vending Machines Australia has sold site-locating to owner-operators since 2006, with no published fee (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/vending-machine-site-location-services, accessed 4 Nov 2026). Package sellers bundle locating into the machine price. A stand-alone locator would compete with both and has no recurring revenue.
- This suits learning the host market, or a later service line (re-siting package buyers' machines). It does not suit a primary business.

### Hybrids
| Hybrid | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A → D (learn, then buy)** | **4** | Learns sites cheaply, then buys distressed package-buyer machines and small routes. Avoids paying 3× asks |

| **B-lite (fridges, 20–100 staff)** | **3** | Under-served size band. Avoid Charter Hall / Morsl estates and 300+-staff sites. (00's "100–300-staff warehouses" overlaps Morsl's logistics push, so I would score that variant 2) |
| **E as bootcamp** | **2** | Pays a premium for training that a 3–5-machine A pilot provides. Non-compete terms unknown |

## 2. The 3 most important findings for the final options

1. **Sites, not machines, are the scarce asset. Every option should be judged on how it acquires sites against "free full service" incumbents.** Only D (and A→D) buys sites already won. A and B-lite must win them on density, service proof and range. F sells sites on 12-month rolling terms, which is the weakest form of a site.
2. **Funded smart-fridge players have moved into industrial estates.** Morsl works through Charter Hall's industrial portfolio and serves logistics clients such as Amazon and Officeworks. Machines Vending and Nouri place micro-markets free. A beginner's fresh-food opening is therefore **smaller sites (20–100 staff), inner-ring offices and residential towers**, not the big western sheds the team has been treating as the best target.
3. **The route-for-sale market is thin and asks are about 3× profit, but there is a hidden supply of cheap assets.** No genuine new Melbourne route has surfaced in 2026. Asking multiples run ~3–3.3× profit, against 07's ≤2–2.5× SDE walk-away. Thousands of placement-package machines (SVA claims 6,000+ sold) on rolling site terms make a pipeline of distressed sellers. A patient buyer can assemble a route from them for much less than broker asks. `ESTIMATE`.
