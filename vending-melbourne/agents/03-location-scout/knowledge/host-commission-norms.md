## Working norms for Melbourne (ESTIMATE)

| Site tier | Typical arrangement | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Small workplace (<50 staff), workshop, gym | **0%**. "Free service" is the pitch. Optionally 5% or a staff discount | Host sees the machine as a staff amenity. Volume too low to share |
| Mid workplace (50–200 staff), childcare, aged care, laundromat, car wash | **5–10% of gross (ex GST)**, paid quarterly or half-yearly | Matches the 10% AU listings |
| Large DC / factory (200+), private hospital, sports club | **10–15%**, or a flat monthly fee where the host demands it | Host has leverage, and operator volume supports it |
| Tendered sites (universities, public hospitals, councils, stations) | **15–30%+ commission, or fixed licence fee / minimum guarantee**, plus compliance obligations | Competitive tenders push the share up. US figures for prime sites hit 20–25%+ |

### Key conventions to adopt
1. **Calculate commission on net sales excluding GST.** Snacks and soft drinks are generally taxable, but some basic foods (e.g. plain water, some milk) are GST-free. Paying commission on GST-inclusive takings over-pays the host by 1/11th. Hand-off to 08 for the GST treatment of the product mix.
2. **Pay from telemetry reports** (cashless platforms such as Nayax support machine-level commission configuration: https://nayax-u.nayax.com/scenario/22348). This gives transparency and builds trust.
3. **Sales threshold clause:** e.g. "commission applies to sales above $X/month" or "no commission in first 3 months", to protect the ramp-up period. Many operators also keep a right to remove the machine if sales fall below a floor (ESTIMATE, common practice).
4. **Term:** 12 months rolling (per the AU listing above), with 30–60 days' termination notice either way.
5. **Electricity:** the host normally absorbs power. A modern refrigerated combo machine uses roughly 5–10 kWh per day, about $1.50–$3.00 per day at ~30c/kWh (ESTIMATE, hand-off to 05 for specific machine figures). This is a hidden "rent" the host pays, so mention it in the pitch.
6. **Alternative to commission:** free product for staff events, a "staff price" on bottled water, or a donation to the host's charity. These are cheaper than cash at low-volume sites.

## Impact on economics (ESTIMATE example)
A machine grossing $300 per week incl. GST equals about $273 ex GST. A 10% commission costs about $27 per week (~$1,420 per year). At typical 45–55% gross margin after product cost (hand-off to 04), commission takes roughly 20% of gross profit. **Commission is a significant, not trivial, line item.** Prefer 0–5% for most early sites.

## Red flags
- Hosts asking for an upfront "placement fee" or fixed rent at small sites. Volume rarely justifies it.
- **Location-locator services** that sell sites to new operators. These are a common source of poor sites in AU and US vending (ESTIMATE). Several AU business-for-sale "turnkey" listings say "locations being finalised", which means the sites are not yet secured (https://bsale.com.au/listing/rare-opportunity-for-melbourne-vic-671667, via search).
- Exclusivity clauses held by an incumbent operator. Ask before pitching.
