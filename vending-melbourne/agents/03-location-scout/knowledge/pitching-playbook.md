# Pitching Playbook: Winning Site Hosts in Melbourne

Mostly practitioner method (**ESTIMATE / best practice**), not cited data. Accessed 2026-11-04.

## 1. Positioning: what the host actually buys
Hosts do not care about the operator's margin. They care about:
- **A staff amenity at no cost.** Operator pages in Melbourne pitch exactly this, e.g. "custom vending machines for FREE" (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/gym-fitness-centre-custom-vending-machines-for-free).
- **Reliability.** Machines never empty, never broken, and refunds handled. Incumbents often fail here, so it is the main switching argument.
- **Cashless payment.** Tap-and-go card and phone payment. Many older machines are coin-only.
- **Night-shift welfare.** Food access for night shifts is a retention and safety talking point for HR.
- **Healthy options**, where relevant (Victorian Healthy Choices framing for health sites).
- **Zero admin.** The host signs one page, and the operator handles everything else.

## 2. Target list building (week 1–2)
1. Choose one cluster (see melbourne-precincts.md).
2. Use Google Maps "warehouse", "distribution centre", "manufacturer", "logistics" plus the suburb, and walk or drive the industrial estate streets. Many sites have no web presence.
3. Use industry networks such as SEMMA (South East Melbourne Manufacturers Alliance, https://semma.com.au/) for Dandenong/Clayton, and local business chambers and council economic development newsletters (Greater Dandenong, Hume, Wyndham, Monash).
4. Watch job ads (Seek, Randstad) for "night shift" and "forklift" in target suburbs. These signal a 24/7 operation and headcount (e.g. https://randstad.com.au/jobs/q-logistics/victoria/truganina/temporary).
5. Look at commercial leasing announcements for new tenants (realcommercial / commercialrealestate "leased" listings). **A new DC fit-out is the best moment to pitch**, because there is no incumbent and the HR team is setting up amenities.

## 3. Channels ranked (ESTIMATE)
| Channel | Conversion | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk-in to reception / site office (industrial) | Highest | Time | Bring a one-page flyer and a photo of the machine. Ask for the site or ops manager |
| Referral from an existing host | High | Free | Ask every happy host for 2 names. Offer a staff-shout bonus |
| LinkedIn to HR / facilities managers | Medium | Low | Good for 100+ staff sites and business parks |
| Cold email | Low | Low | Follow up by phone or visit |
| Property / facilities managers (business parks, BTR) | Medium, but multi-site | Low | One "yes" can unlock several buildings |
| Tenders (universities, hospitals, councils) | Low for beginners | High | Monitor Tenders VIC (https://www.tenders.vic.gov.au/) and australiantenders.com.au (e.g. Melbourne Polytechnic tender: https://www.australiantenders.com.au/tenders/611656/vending-machines) |

## 4. Walk-in script (≈60 seconds)
> "Hi, I'm [Name] from [Business]. We're a local Melbourne vending operator. We put modern tap-and-pay snack and drink machines into workplaces in [Dandenong South] at no cost to you. We stock, clean and fix it, and we're usually back within 24 hours if anything goes wrong. Who looks after staff amenities here? Can I leave this one-pager and come back Thursday to show them?"

## 5. Meeting pitch structure
1. **Discovery:** headcount, shifts, current food options, complaints, existing machine.
2. **Offer:** machine type (combo / drinks / smart fridge), products tailored to their staff (ask for a staff wishlist), cashless payment and a refund QR code.
3. **Service commitment (SLA, a service-level agreement):** restock X times a week, fault response within 24 to 48 hours, monthly sales report on request.
4. **Commercials:** default 0%. Offer 5–10% only if headcount justifies it (see host-commission-norms.md).
5. **Risk reversal:** "Trial for 90 days. If you're not happy, we remove it at our cost."
6. **Close:** a one-page placement agreement with term, commission, power, access, insurance, removal clause. Hand-off to 08 for the template.

## 6. Objection handling
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already have a machine." | "How often is it empty or broken? Does it take card? We can do a side-by-side or take over when the contract ends." |
| "We don't want junk food." | "We'll set the mix. We can run a healthier range, including water, protein and lower-sugar options, aligned to Victoria's Healthy Choices guidelines." |
| "What does it cost us?" | "Nothing except a power point, about the cost of a bar fridge." (Give a kWh ESTIMATE from 05.) |
| "Who's liable if it breaks / someone gets hurt?" | "We carry $20M public liability insurance and the machine is ours." (Confirm the insurance level with 08.) |
| "Our landlord / head office decides." | "Happy to send a one-page proposal to them. Who should I address it to?" |
| "What's in it for us?" | Staff amenity, retention, a night-shift welfare story. Optional commission or charity donation. |

## 7. Leave-behind kit
- One-page flyer: photo, product examples, "free placement, cashless, local service" and a phone number.
- One-page agreement.
- Insurance certificate of currency (public liability).
- References or testimonials, once you have them. Until then, offer the trial.
- Optional: a staff product-wishlist card.

## 8. Retention: keeping sites
- Keep it full. Never let best-sellers run out (telemetry: hand-off to 05).
- Keep it clean. Use a visible refund QR code and resolve refunds the same day.
- Send a quarterly check-in and rotate products.
- Pay commission on time with a clear statement.
- Ask for referrals at the 3-month mark.

## 9. Realistic funnel (ESTIMATE)
Out of 100 industrial walk-ins, expect about 30 conversations with a decision-maker, about 10 site assessments and 3–6 placements. Plan 6–10 weeks to secure the first 5 sites. These conversion rates are unverified and should be tracked from day one.
