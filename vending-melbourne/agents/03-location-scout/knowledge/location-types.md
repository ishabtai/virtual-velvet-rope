## Jargon (explained once)
- **Site host / location:** the business or organisation whose premises hold the machine.
- **Placement:** one machine at one site.
- **Commission / rebate:** the share of sales the operator pays the host. In Australian listings this is often called a "rebate".
- **Captive audience:** people who cannot easily get food elsewhere, such as night-shift warehouse staff or hospital visitors at 2am. This is the main driver of vending sales.
- **Micro-market / smart fridge:** an unattended fridge or open-shelf store with card or app payment. It suits closed workplaces of roughly 50+ staff.
- **Route:** a group of placements serviced on one restocking run.
- **Traffic-light (red/amber/green) rules:** Victorian Government food classification used in hospitals, health services and schools. "Red" means least healthy.

## Core principle
Sales are driven by **captive headcount × hours of access × lack of alternatives**, not by raw foot traffic. Operator marketing makes the same point: the location matters more than the machine, and "a small workshop might outperform a big retail store" (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/why-location-matters-more-than-the-machine-the-real-strategy-behind-profitable-used-vending-machines, via search snippet). The same operator recommends areas with "high foot traffic and a lack of available food and beverage options" and sites with seating areas (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/a-good-place-to-install-vending-machines, via search snippet).

## Detail by type

### 1. Warehouses, distribution centres and factories (top beginner target)
- Melbourne's industrial nodes are large. The Dandenong NEIC employs **66,300+** people (https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0031/628366/plan-melbourne-map-dandenong-national-employment-innovation-cluster.pdf, via search snippet). Dandenong has almost **1,400 manufacturing businesses and 21,000+ manufacturing workers** (https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-08/220817%20-%20Manufacturing%20The%20Future%20In%20Dandenong.pdf; 2022, may be stale).
- Western logistics: Truganina is described as part of the "Western Industrial Node, one of Melbourne's three major industrial areas" (real-estate listing via search; promotional). Job ads in Truganina and Laverton North are dominated by forklift, logistics and production roles at about $36–$50 per hour (Randstad listings, e.g. https://randstad.com.au/jobs/q-logistics/victoria/truganina/temporary). That points to shift-based blue-collar staff, who are core vending buyers.
- Why they work: 24/7 rosters, short breaks (often 30 minutes), long distances to shops, and often no canteen.
- Watch-outs: big 3PLs (third-party logistics companies) may run national procurement. Night-shift staff favour energy drinks, hot food and cheap staples. Cashless payment is mandatory. The machine must be on site in a lunchroom, not public-facing.
- Ideal minimum: about 40+ staff on site across shifts, or 25+ if the site is 24/7 (ESTIMATE, based on US rule-of-thumb "50 employees per snack/drink combo" scaled for the 24/7 premium).

### 2. Hospitals
- Victorian public health services must follow **Healthy Choices: policy directive for Victorian public health services**, introduced in 2021. Red (sugary) drinks were to be removed from vending and in-house retail by **30 September 2022**, and the targets continue under the January 2025 update (https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices-policy-directive-victorian-public-health-services; https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/healthy-choices-policy-directive-for-victorian-public-health-services-short-summary-2025.pdf, via search snippets).
- One summary cites vending targets of "at least 50% GREEN drinks and no more than 20% [amber]…" (truncated; confirm with 04/08).
- Monash Health reports all vending machines are compliant, with red drinks removed and artificially sweetened amber drinks reduced (https://coronavirus.monashhealth.org/latest-news/2022/12/02/more-healthy-choices-at-work/). Alfred Health removed sugary drinks at Caulfield and Sandringham (https://www.alfredhealth.org.au/news/sandringham-and-caulfield-hospitals-cut-back-on-sugar).
- Implication: public hospitals are high-volume but **tendered, compliance-heavy and lower-margin** on the "healthy" range. Private hospitals and private aged care are **not bound** by the directive (they may adopt it voluntarily), but public-sector residential aged care services (PSRACS) and integrated community health services **are** bound (verified 2026-11-04 via search summary of https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices). Private sites are the easier entry points.

### 3. Universities and TAFE
- These are huge populations. Monash University (Clayton) sits in a cluster of about 75,000–95,000 jobs (see melbourne-precincts.md). The University of Melbourne has 55,000+ students (https://www.vic.gov.au/parkville-precinct).
- Procurement is formal. **Melbourne Polytechnic** went to tender to "supply, install, operate, and maintain food and beverage vending machines across all campuses", with the notice released 19 Sep 2025 (https://www.australiantenders.com.au/tenders/611656/vending-machines).
- Sales are seasonal: semester weeks are strong, and summer (Dec–Feb) is weak.
- Beginner route: target **independent residential colleges, student accommodation (PBSA) buildings and private colleges** rather than the main campus contract.

### 4. 24/7 gyms
- Melbourne is dense with 24/7 franchise gyms (Anytime Fitness, Snap, Jetts, Plus Fitness, etc.).
- Products: water, sports drinks, protein, healthy snacks, iced coffee and recovery drinks (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/gym-fitness-centre-custom-vending-machines-for-free, via snippet).
- Competitor watch: **Fitness Vending**, a wholesale sports-nutrition supplier to gyms across Australasia that supplies networks including Anytime Fitness, is FITAID's exclusive AU distributor (https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/fitness-vending-announces-fitaid-partnership). Some gyms already have a supplier or sell from the counter.
- Economics: membership counts are moderate. Gyms are good as a **cluster filler**, not an anchor.

### 5. Offices
- CBD and Docklands host large employers (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/docklands-vending-machines, operator page). Competition from cafés is intense, though, and hybrid work reduces Monday/Friday occupancy (ESTIMATE).
- Better: suburban offices and business parks with no café in walking distance, such as Clayton/Monash business parks, Tullamarine/Airport business park and Mulgrave.
- Smart fridges or micro-markets suit offices of 80+ staff (hand-off to 05).

### 6. Apartments and build-to-rent (BTR)
- BTR towers (single professional landlord) make decisions faster than strata body corporates.
- Niche: convenience, late-night snacks, household items. Moderate volume.
- Strata buildings need an owners corporation resolution. Expect delays of months.

### 7. Car washes and laundromats
- Dwell-time sites: customers wait 10–60 minutes.
- Product mix is partly non-food (car fragrance, cloths, detergent). Hand-off to 04.
- Owners often already stock a machine themselves. Pitch as a "we run it, you get X% with no work" offer.

### 8. Transport hubs
- Footscray Station and interchange appear in operator target lists (https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/footscray-vending-machines). However, metropolitan train-station vending is governed by network concession or retail-leasing arrangements. **Not a beginner site.** Hand-off to 08 to confirm who controls station retail.

### 9. Sports clubs and council leisure centres
- Community clubs (football/cricket/netball) are volunteer-run, seasonal and very receptive. Council aquatic and leisure centres are often run by contractors (YMCA, Belgravia, Aligned Leisure, etc.) and may tender vending (ESTIMATE).
- Some councils apply healthy-choices guidelines in their own facilities (Rethink Sugary Drink guidelines: https://www.rethinksugarydrink.org.au/downloads/workplaces/healthy-vending-machine-guidelines.pdf).

### 10. Schools (generally avoid)
- Victorian government schools must follow the **Canteens, Healthy Eating and Other Food Services Policy** (updated 25 June 2024, reviewed 25 July 2025), which uses traffic-light classification (https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/canteens-and-healthy-eating/policy). Guidance says red drinks should not be in vending in child or adolescent settings (Rethink Sugary Drink guidelines). Low margins and high compliance risk. Possible exceptions are staff rooms and independent-school staff areas.

## Ranking for a zero-machine beginner (ESTIMATE, qualitative)
1. Warehouses / DCs / factories in industrial nodes (Dandenong South, Laverton North/Truganina, Tullamarine, Campbellfield)
2. Small workshops, trade businesses and mechanics as **first test placements** (easy yes, low volume)
3. 24/7 gyms and BTR apartments as cluster fillers
4. Private hospitals, aged care, childcare (rebate expected)
5. Suburban offices and business parks (smart fridge if 80+ staff)
6. Laundromats and car washes (niche mix)
7. Universities, public hospitals, stations, council leisure centres: **tender-only**, later stage
