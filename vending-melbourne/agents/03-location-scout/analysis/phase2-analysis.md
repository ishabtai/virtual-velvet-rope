# Phase 2 Analysis: Location Scout (03)

Lens: **how easy is it to win and keep suitable Greater Melbourne sites under each business-model archetype.** Date: 2026-11-04. AUD. This round used 8 web searches; page fetches were not attempted for domains already known to be blocked. Evidence below comes from search-result summaries unless stated.

Scale: 5 = sites are easy to win and sticky; 1 = sites are hard to win or likely to be lost.

## 1. Context that drives every score

- **The host offer is identical everywhere.** Every Melbourne operator, from Coke Vending to one-person locals, offers a free machine, install, restock and maintenance (02 `gaps-and-positioning.md`). VENHAR (Melbourne West) advertises "optional commission… if you'd like one" and names warehouses and DCs as ideal sites (https://sites.google.com/view/venharvending/home, accessed 2026-11-04). So **winning a small site is easy, but nothing stops the next operator taking it.** Retention rests on service quality, not on contracts.
- **Contracts are short.** The AU norm is a 12-month rolling agreement with 30–60 days' notice (`knowledge/host-commission-norms.md`). Any model whose value depends on "owning" sites rests on weak legal ground.
- **Route density beats site quality** (`knowledge/melbourne-precincts.md`). A model that hands you scattered sites is penalised here even if each site is acceptable.
- **The new-site pipeline is shrinking slightly.** Knight Frank forecasts ~584,000 sqm of new Melbourne industrial supply in 2026, ~20% below 2025. Q1 2026 completions (~75,000 sqm) were mostly in the South-East, and ESR's speculative Enterprise Industry Park in Pakenham added 39,000 sqm in Q2 2026 (https://propertymarkets.news/melbournes-industrial-market-to-strengthen-in-2026/ ; https://www.cbre.com.au/insights/figures/melbourne-industrial-and-logistics-figures-q1-2026 ; https://content.knightfrank.com/research/2867/documents/en/melbourne-industrial-state-of-the-market-q2-2026-12931.pdf, all accessed 2026-11-04). New fit-outs, the best pitch moment, are still happening but more in the South-East (Dandenong South to Pakenham) than the West in 2026.

## 2. Scores by archetype

| # | Archetype | Win | Keep | **Score** | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Classic DIY route | 4 | 3 | **3.5** | Small/mid sites say yes to a free service at 0–10%, but they can be poached |
| B | Smart fridge / micro-market | 2 | 4 | **3** (B-lite fridge 3.5; full micro-market 2.5) | Few qualifying sites and well-funded rivals, but very sticky once installed |
| C | Niche / specialty | 2 | 2 | **2** | Needs high-footfall public sites run by landlords or tenders; novelty churn |
| D | Buy existing route | 5 | 3 | **4** | Sites on day one; real risk of losing some on change of owner |
| E | Franchise / licence | 4 | 3 | **3** | Franchisor supplies sites and national contracts, but you may not control them |
| F | Placement package | 3 | 2 | **2** | Sites "secured" but unverified, scattered, re-site terms undisclosed |
| G | Placement-only / locator | 3 | 1 | **1.5** | Site-winning is the whole job, but retention depends on someone else's service |

### A. Classic DIY route: 3.5
- **Win (4):** The best beginner targets are warehouses, factories and trade sites with 25–200 staff (`knowledge/location-types.md`). These hosts face no cost and usually accept 0–10% commission. The funnel ESTIMATE is 100 industrial walk-ins → ~30 decision-maker conversations → ~10 assessments → 3–6 placements, with 6–10 weeks to secure the first 5 sites (`knowledge/pitching-playbook.md` §9, unverified).
- **Keep (3):** Rolling 12-month terms, and incumbents (SVA, VENHAR, Vending Machines Australia) work the same West and South-East belts (02 `local-operators.md`). Retention depends on restock reliability, telemetry-based restocking and fault response.
- **Advantage:** You choose every site, so you can build one dense cluster. No other archetype offers this.

### B. Smart fridge / micro-market: 3 (B-lite 3.5, full micro-market 2.5)
- **Win (2):** Qualifying sites are fewer. You need a closed, secure workplace with roughly 80–150+ staff for a micro-market (overseas vendor thresholds: 50, 80, 150 and 200; https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/micro-markets-operator-guide ; https://connectvending.co.uk/micro-markets-solution, accessed 2026-11-04). Large warehouses are already being chased by funded players. Morsl (Series A, $5m) had 13 established NSW/VIC sites, a pipeline of 20+ new sites, and named large warehouses and logistics firms as clients (https://www.forbes.com.au/news/entrepreneurs/morsl-adds-5-million-in-series-a-raise/ ; https://c-store.com.au/?p=58157, accessed 2026-11-04). Hosts at that size often go through procurement or facilities management, which means longer sales cycles.
- **Keep (4):** Once a fridge or market is installed and staff rely on it for meals, removing it is visible and unpopular. Switching costs are higher than for a spiral machine.
- **B-lite** (single smart fridges in 40–150-staff sites) scores higher. 02 found 20–100-staff workplaces under-served by fresh-food formats, and those sites are easier to win than the 300+-staff sites that Ausbox and Morsl target.

### C. Niche / specialty: 2
- Novelty concepts (pizza, gacha, Asian snacks) need public footfall: stations, shopping centres, campuses. These are controlled by landlords, tenders or concession contracts. I found no evidence of who controls Metro Trains station vending. Metro's franchise expires in late 2027 and is being re-tendered, so station retail arrangements may change (search summary, https://railwaynews.net/?p=245764, accessed 2026-11-04). Sydney Metro ran a formal vending RFT (https://www.australiantenders.com.au/tenders/549317/rft-sydney-metro-city-and-southwest-csw-food-and-beverage-vending-machines). The pattern is tendered and beyond beginner reach.
- **Exception:** "Workplace niche" (PPE, tools or energy drinks in industrial estates) is easy to place because it rides on A's sites. Score it as part of A + C, not as standalone C.

### D. Buy an existing route: 4
- **Win (5):** You buy existing placements with sales history. Melbourne examples: 11 machines for $100k with rolling contracts (https://bsale.com.au/listing/rare-opportunity-for-melbourne-vic-671667), and a 62-machine network (under offer).
- **Keep (3):** Sites are personal relationships on rolling 12-month terms, and hosts can leave when the owner changes. **ESTIMATE: plan for 10–20% of sites lost or needing renegotiation in the first 12 months after purchase.** This is reasoning, not data; there is no AU source. Assignability and a seller handover period (introductions to every host) are the key diligence items (hand-off to 08).
- The best location-lens fit is a route that is concentrated in one cluster. A scattered route scores closer to F.

### E. Franchise / licence: 3
- **Win (4):** Provender says its Franchise Management Office helps franchisees identify new sites, and a Geelong resale lists national contracts with gyms, hospitals, government departments and national retailers (https://commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/21052-premier-vending-franchise-opportunity-in-geelong-profitable-existing-sites-2019832182 ; https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/franchise/provender/3097, accessed 2026-11-04). A recognised brand helps with corporate and procurement-led sites that a new independent would struggle to win.
- **Keep (3):** National contracts may belong to the franchisor, not to you. Territory limits stop you chasing sites outside your area. Check in the franchise disclosure document who holds each site agreement and what happens to those sites when you exit.

### F. Placement package: 2
- **Win (3, in theory):** Sites come pre-arranged. But AU listings still say "locations being finalised" (https://bsale.com.au/listing/rare-opportunity-for-melbourne-vic-671667) or give no history.
- **Keep (2):** Royal Vending packages offer a re-site option **only if weekly takings fall below an undisclosed threshold in the first 180 days**, plus a replacement if a site is lost "through no fault of your own" within 180 days. Terms are available only on request (https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/vending-machines-with-placement-re-site-option-geelong/762257, accessed 2026-11-04). After day 180 the buyer carries all site risk. I found no independent buyer reviews.
- **Density penalty:** Package sites are typically spread across Melbourne (02 `routes-for-sale.md`), which breaks route density.

### G. Placement-only / asset-light: 1.5
- A locator is paid to win sites but has no control over service. Sites placed with weak operators churn, and the locator's reputation suffers. Locator-supplied sites are a known source of poor AU/US placements (`knowledge/host-commission-norms.md`, red flags). This is viable only as a side activity that feeds your own route.

### Hybrids
| Hybrid | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A → D (learn, then buy) | **4.5** | Build a cluster yourself, then buy a nearby small or underperforming route to densify. Your own track record helps retain the acquired hosts |
| A + C (classic plus niche anchor) | 3.5 | Niche lines sit inside A's sites. A standalone public-footfall niche site stays hard |
| B-lite (smart fridges in 100–300-staff warehouses) | 3 | Win rate falls above ~150 staff because of Morsl, Ausbox, Coke&Go and procurement. Best at 40–150 staff |
| E as bootcamp | 3 | Brand access helps, but non-compete and site ownership may block taking sites with you |

## 3. Three most important findings for the final options

1. **Site acquisition is a service game, not a contract game.** Every rival offers the same free deal on rolling 12-month terms. Any option must budget the founder's time for continuous prospecting (~3–6 placements per 100 walk-ins, ESTIMATE) and for retention through reliability. Models that "include sites" (D, E, F) do not remove this; they only change the starting stock.
2. **Concentrate in one cluster, and favour the South-East for 2026 new-site leads.** Route density drives the economics (07's lone-machine vs route cases). New 2026 industrial completions are concentrated in the South-East (Dandenong South, Pakenham). Archetypes that give you scattered sites (F, most G, some D) are structurally weaker.
3. **The 40–150-staff closed workplace is the sweet spot.** It is big enough for a combo machine or a single smart fridge, too small for Morsl, Ausbox and Coke, and decided by one site manager rather than procurement. A, B-lite and A → D fit this best. Full micro-markets (150+) and public footfall sites (stations, campuses, public hospitals) are tender or procurement territory. Defer them to year 2+.

## 5. Conflicts with other agents' knowledge bases

1. **Weekly gross per machine (03 vs 07).** My `location-types.md` gives warehouses $250–$800 per week. 07 uses base $175 and high $300, backed by a 45-machine Melbourne listing (~$184/week). The 62-machine claim (~$350/week) sits in between. **07's figures are better evidenced.** I now treat my ranges as upside cases for top-decile sites and have added a note to `location-types.md`.
2. **00 says micro-markets suit "hospitals".** From the location lens, public hospitals are tendered and Healthy Choices-bound (now confirmed mandatory for public services only). Private hospitals and private aged care are the accessible health targets.
3. **00 (A) "kills it: fewer than ~50 people a day"** vs my 25+ staff threshold for 24/7 sites. These are compatible: 25 staff on rotating 24/7 shifts generate more than 50 visits per day. State it as headcount × shifts, not raw headcount.
4. **02 says the 20–100-staff segment is under-served; 00's B-lite targets 100–300.** Location evidence favours 02's range (40–150) for winnability. 05/07 should check that the smart-fridge economics close at the lower end.
5. **Truganina headcount.** 00 and 02 treat the West as a major cluster. Council planning projects 10,000–15,000 new jobs in the Truganina Employment Precinct (a projection, not a count), and I still have no current place-of-work job count. Treat the West as strong but less quantified than Dandenong (66,300+ jobs).
