# Business-Model Archetypes for a Melbourne Vending Start-up

Owner: 00 Lead Strategist · Researched: 4 Nov 2026 · All prices AUD unless stated.

> **Method note.** Research was done with web search. Direct page fetching was blocked by this session's network policy, so the facts below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, accessed 4 Nov 2026. Broker listings and vendor blogs are sales material: treat their numbers as claims, not evidence. Figures marked **ESTIMATE** are my own reasoning and are explained.

## Jargon used once

- **Route / run:** the set of machines one operator services on a regular loop. "Buying a route" means buying the machines plus the site agreements.
- **Site / location / host:** the business that lets you put a machine on its premises.
- **Commission / rebate:** the share of sales paid to the host, usually a % of gross sales.
- **Placement / locator service:** a company that finds sites for you, often sold bundled with machines.
- **Micro-market:** an unstaffed mini-shop (open shelves plus fridges) with a self-checkout kiosk, usually in a workplace.
- **Smart fridge:** a locked fridge that unlocks after you tap a card. Weight sensors or cameras then charge you for what you take.
- **SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings):** profit available to one owner-operator, after adding back the owner's wage and personal expenses. Businesses for sale are priced as a multiple of SDE.
- **Cashless telemetry:** a card reader plus SIM (e.g. Nayax) that takes card payments and reports sales and stock levels remotely.
- **WIWO:** "walk in, walk out". The price includes everything needed to keep trading.

## A. Classic snack/drink route (DIY)

**What it is.** You buy new or used spiral/combo machines, find your own sites, stock them from wholesalers (e.g. Costco, Campbells, NQR-type clearance stores) and service them weekly.

**Evidence**
- New machines cost roughly $7k–$15k and used ones $1k–$4k, per Vending Machines Australia (vendor). Typical used prices: snack $2,000–$3,500, drink $2,500–$4,000. https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/vending-machines-used-a-smart-low-risk-way-to-start-a-vending-business
- A Sydney supplier quotes new combo machines at $6,890–$8,790 (via realcommercial.com.au). https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/sad-reality-of-teens-12k-side-hustle
- Initial stock per machine: $200–$400 (US-origin guide). https://moneyinc.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-vending-machine-business-in-2026/
- A quoted Australian operator estimate: about $650–$750 gross sales per machine per month, and about $400–$500 net profit per month. https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/sad-reality-of-teens-12k-side-hustle
- For comparison, a US survey of 23 operators found an average fleet of 13 machines grossing US$309 per machine per month (The Hustle). https://thehustle.co/the-economics-of-vending-machines
- Typical Australian host commission is 10% of sales, paid every 6 months (seen across many listings). The general industry range is 5–25%. https://bsale.com.au/listing/new-vending-business-melbourne-vic-687018 ; https://vendingexchange.com/vending-machine-locators/vending-location-commission-explained

**ESTIMATE, unit economics for a decent Melbourne site:** $700 per month gross ex GST × (1 − 45% cost of goods − 10% commission − 3–5% card fees and telemetry) ≈ $280–$320 per month contribution before vehicle, time, depreciation and insurance. The $400–$500 net figure quoted above looks optimistic unless margins are above 60%. **Hand-off to 07 to model properly.**

**Fits:** a founder with a small budget, low risk tolerance, and a wish to learn the business hands-on.
**Kills it:** spending a lot on new machines before sites are proven; low-traffic sites (fewer than ~50 people a day).

## B. Smart fridge / micro-market (workplace unattended retail)

**What it is.** A fresh food and drinks fridge, or a mini-market with a kiosk, inside an office, warehouse, hospital, gym or apartment building. The basket per sale is bigger (meals, not just a Mars bar), but it needs more service.

**Evidence**
- Morsl calls itself Australia's first unattended micro-market operator and says it serves 5,000+ employees across NSW and VIC (aggregator profile, unverified). https://bouncewatch.com/company/provender ; https://yespress.io/morsl
- 365 Retail Markets launched its MicroMarket concept in Australia at the AVA Melbourne show in July 2018. https://365retailmarkets.com/blog/365-retail-markets-expands-micromarket-technology-australia
- Provender sells micro-market franchises. Asking prices: a Dandenong VIC micro-market "from $70,000 + GST" and a Brisbane one "from $140,000 + GST and stock". https://www.commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/21052-premium-micro-market-unattended-retail-franchise-business-dandenong-2020004398 ; https://bsale.com.au/listing/21052--premium-micro-brisbane-qld-677643
- US benchmarks: a 125-employee site makes more than US$1,000 a week (Parlevel). The typical range is US$1k–$3k a week (Bernick's). The minimum viable site is 80 staff (UK supplier) or 150 staff (another operator guide). https://bernicks.com/blog/micro-market-sales-revenue ; https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/micro-markets-operator-guide
- Office commissions are typically 10–15% of gross (US). https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/28/office-micro-market-bookkeeping-shrinkage-inventory-commission-guide

**Fits:** a founder with more capital, food-handling willingness, and B2B sales skills. Suits the logistics and industrial belts in the west and south-east (Dandenong, Truganina, Derrimut, Laverton) and hospitals. Hospitals must follow the Victorian Healthy Choices directive: at least 50% GREEN items and no more than 20% RED items, and combo machines may carry no RED drinks. https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices
**Kills it:** small sites, high shrinkage (theft), and fresh-food waste. Food-safety obligations also apply (**hand-off to 08**).

## C. Niche / specialty vending

**What it is.** A distinctive machine or concept: hot pizza, gachapon (capsule) toys, claw machines, Asian snacks, PPE or tools on industrial sites, local produce, flowers, phone chargers.

**Evidence**
- A gourmet pizza vending machine (Me Group, 600+ units across Europe and Japan) popped up at Southern Cross Station in Aug 2023 as a temporary placement. https://timeout.com/melbourne/news/australias-only-gourmet-pizza-vending-machine-has-just-popped-up-at-southern-cross-station-080823
- Pizza Gio in Sydney (2014) closed soon after launch. Aldi's Pizzabot (North Sydney, 2022) was a retailer's own experiment. This history shows novelty is fragile. https://thebrag.com/australias-first-pizza-vending-machine/ ; https://www.smartcompany.com.au/industries/retail/aldi-pizza-robot/
- Capsule Station, a 24-hour gacha machine store, operates on Level 2 of Melbourne Central. https://timeout.com/melbourne/shopping/capsule-station
- Vending Times (16 Jun 2026) reports concerns in Australia about Japanese-style gacha machines aimed at children. This is a potential regulatory or reputational risk. https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/japanese-style-vending-machines-spark-concerns-in-australia/
- A 62-machine Melbourne route already carries PPE and fresh food alongside snacks, so "niche" lines can sit inside a classic route. https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/under-offer-expansive-network-of-62-food-vending-machines-rev-95k-p-m/746904

**Fits:** a founder with a product angle or a brand. Can be layered on top of archetype A later.
**Kills it:** a high equipment price (hot-food robots can cost tens of thousands, **ESTIMATE**, see 05) paired with demand that hasn't been tested. Concept fatigue is a second risk.

## D. Buy an existing route

**What it is.** You acquire machines, site agreements, sales history and sometimes a van.

**Evidence (Melbourne and comparable)**
- LINK Melbourne listing: 62 food machines across Melbourne, trading since 2008, revenue over $95k a month, 65–69% gross margin, "$1.2M asset value", price on application, **under offer**. https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/under-offer-expansive-network-of-62-food-vending-machines-rev-95k-p-m/746904
- A Melbourne vending run listed at $500,000, self-managed (AnyBusiness, updated Jun 2024, possibly stale). https://ftp.anybusiness.com.au/listings/melbourne-vic-3000-leisure-entertainment-vending-3423035
- 11 machines, Melbourne, $100k ex GST, rolling site contracts, operating 15+ years. https://bsale.com.au/listing/rare-opportunity-for-melbourne-vic-671667
- Brisbane: 60 machines across 36 sites, $600,000 WIWO, all machines owned, revenue not disclosed. https://linkbusiness.com.au/business-for-sale/br01993/
- Valuation: US vending businesses sold at a median 2.15x SDE (middle half 1.8–2.62x), and route brokers quote 2.0–3.5x. General Australian SMEs trade at 1.5–5x SDE, mostly 2–3x. https://bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/vending-machine-business ; https://www.businessforsale.com.au/business-advice/how-much-is-my-business-worth-a-general-guide-for-australian-owners

**Fits:** a founder with capital or finance who wants cash flow from day one.
**Kills it:** buying without seeing the telemetry and bank deposits, or without checking that the site agreements can be assigned to a new owner. Paying more than 3x SDE for a route with short-term contracts.

## E. Franchise / licence

**Evidence**
- Provender is a national franchise covering vending, micro-markets and coffee (since 2005 or 2008, depending on the source). Geelong vending resale "from $40,000 + GST". Insurance $1,500–$5,000 a year, plus working capital from $5,000. https://commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/21052-premier-vending-franchise-opportunity-in-geelong-profitable-existing-sites-2019832182 ; https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/franchise/provender/3097
- Ausbox Vending Group (Melbourne, since 2004) sells a licence or distribution package. One Altona listing gives the investment as $34,980–$36,980, focused on offices, government and warehouses. https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/credit-card-vending-machine-business-ausbox-vending-group-altona-350-staff/709307
- Just Now Vending: from $50,000, no royalties (listing). https://www.business2sell.com.au/franchise-details/just-now-vending.php

**Fits:** a founder who wants training and a brand that corporate clients recognise.
**Kills it:** paying a franchise premium for sites you could have found yourself. Ask for the disclosure document required under the Franchising Code (**hand-off to 08**).

## F. Placement package (new machines + "secured sites")

**Evidence**
- Melbourne listings: 5 cashless machines plus 5 site contracts for $30k + GST, with no trading history. Another: 5 SVA2000 cashless machines, 10% rebate to the site, $30k + GST. Another: 2 machines with Nayax readers and pre-secured sites for $20k + GST. https://bsale.com.au/listing/new-vending-business-melbourne-vic-684863 ; https://bsale.com.au/listing/new-vending-business-melbourne-vic-687018 ; https://www.businessforsale.com.au/australia/JBS5-391590/new-vending-business-for-sale-grow-your-own-vending-business-with-our-support
- Royal Vending packages include a 24-month warranty and a 180-day re-site option (SEEK snippet).
- Risk pattern: the ACCC pursued a juicer "business opportunity" in which pre-arranged sites and profits were promised and not delivered. US DOJ and FTC cases show the same pattern with vending placement. https://www.accc.gov.au/node/14733 ; https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/seven-individuals-charged-manhattan-federal-court-connection-multimillion-dollar

**ESTIMATE:** $30k for 5 machines = $6k per machine, about $1.5–3k above buying used machines and finding sites yourself. The package premium is effectively the price of the sites and the support. It is worth paying only if the sites come with traffic data and a re-site guarantee.

## G. Placement-only / asset-light

- **Locator:** you sell site-finding to other operators. Low capital, but you need a network.
- **Host:** you take commission on someone else's machine. Not a business for this founder.
- **Hybrid:** you lease or rent-to-own machines, or run machines on a fixed-rent site.
Useful mainly as a **side income while you learn**, or as an exit option.

## Hybrids worth carrying into Phase 2

1. **A → D (learn, then buy):** run 3–5 used machines for 6–12 months, then buy a small route (10–20 machines) that is underperforming.
2. **A + C (classic plus niche anchor):** a classic route funds one signature niche machine (e.g. Asian snacks near universities).
3. **B-lite:** smart fridges only (no full micro-market) in 100–300-staff warehouses in the west and south-east.
4. **E as bootcamp:** buy a franchise territory to get training and corporate access, then expand with your own machines if the agreement allows it (check non-compete terms).
