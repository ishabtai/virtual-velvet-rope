# Decision Framework: Choosing and Testing a Vending Model

Owner: 00 Lead Strategist · 4 Nov 2026 · AUD.

The founder hasn't fixed **budget, time or niche**. This framework turns those three unknowns into a short list of options, then gives each option a 90-day test with go/no-go gates. Scores are **ESTIMATES** for the team to revise in Phase 2.

---

## Step 1: Founder profile (three dials)

| Dial | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital available** (cash + finance) | < $15k | $15k–$80k | $80k–$600k |
| **Weekly hours** | < 8 h (side hustle) | 8–25 h | Full-time (35+ h) |
| **Risk appetite** | Can't lose more than ~$5k | Can lose ~$20k | Can lose $100k+ / borrow |

Additional filters:
- Willing to handle **fresh food**? (Opens archetype B; triggers food-safety rules. See 08.)
- Has a **vehicle** that can carry 100+ kg of stock? A van becomes needed at about 10+ machines (ESTIMATE).
- Comfortable doing **B2B sales** (cold-calling facility managers)?
- Has a **network** (employer, gym, uni, industrial park contacts)?

## Step 3: Weighted scoring

Criteria and suggested weights (re-weight to match the founder's priorities):

| Criterion | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Capital required | Cash to reach ~10 machines or equivalent | 20% |
| Time to first profit | Months until monthly contribution > $0 after all costs | 15% |
| Owner hours per $ earned | Implied hourly rate at the target scale | 15% |
| Downside risk | Loss if it fails (resale value of machines vs sunk cost) | 20% |
| Scalability | Can it reach 50+ units or $150k+ SDE? | 15% |
| Defensibility | Contracts, brand, relationships, exclusivity | 10% |
| Evidence strength | How well Melbourne data supports the case | 5% |

## Step 4: Hard gates (any "no" eliminates the option)

1. **Can the founder lose the whole stake and be OK?** If not, start no bigger than A with 1–3 used machines.
2. **For D:** does the seller provide ≥12 months of telemetry or bank deposits, plus site agreements that can be assigned to a buyer? If not, walk away.
3. **For E/F:** does the seller provide the disclosure document (franchise), named sites with foot-traffic data, and a written re-site guarantee? If not, walk away.
4. **For B:** is there at least one site with ≥80–150 staff on site who lack nearby food options? If not, don't proceed. https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/micro-markets-operator-guide
5. **For health-service sites:** can the product mix meet Victorian Healthy Choices (≥50% GREEN, ≤20% RED, no RED drinks in combo machines)? https://heas.health.vic.gov.au/healthy-choices
6. **Unit test:** at expected sales, does contribution per machine exceed $150 a month after cost of goods, commission, card fees and telemetry (ESTIMATE threshold)? If not, re-site or stop.

## Step 5: 90-day test plans

### A. Classic DIY (budget ≈ $5k–$12k)
| Days | Actions | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| 0–14 | ABN; public liability insurance; buy 1–2 used combo machines with cashless readers; set up wholesale accounts | Machines bought for ≤ $4k each |
| 15–30 | Pitch 30 sites (warehouses, gyms, car washes, trade schools); place 2 machines | ≥2 signed sites, ≤10% commission |
| 31–90 | Service weekly; rotate the slowest 20% of products; log time and kilometres | Gross ≥ $500/month per machine; owner time ≤ 1.5 h per machine per week |
| **Gate** | GO: ≥1 machine at $600+/month and positive contribution → scale to 5. PIVOT: re-site. STOP: both below $300/month after re-siting. | |

### B. Smart fridge (budget ≈ $15k–$30k)
| Days | Actions | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 | Sign 1 anchor site (150+ staff, no canteen); lease or buy 1 smart fridge; register the food business with council (08) | Letter of intent signed |
| 31–60 | Launch with 40–60 products; service 2–3 times a week | Weekly sales; shrinkage % |
| 61–90 | Tune the range; pitch 2 more sites using the data | Sales ≥ $800/week; shrinkage < 3%; waste < 8% (ESTIMATE targets) |
| **Gate** | GO: targets met → 3–5 fridges. STOP: < $400/week. | |

### C. Niche (budget varies)
- Days 0–30: demand test without buying a machine. Run a pop-up table or honour box, or rent a machine for a month. Use social media pre-orders.
- Days 31–90: one machine at one venue partner, on a short contract.
- Gate: sales at month 3 ≥ 70% of month 1 (shows the novelty isn't fading) and payback < 18 months.

### D. Buy a route
- Days 0–30: screen 5+ listings; NDA; request telemetry, BAS (quarterly GST statements) and site contracts. Accountant reviews SDE.
- Days 31–60: ride along on the route; contact 3+ sites (with the seller's permission); inspect machine condition.
- Days 61–90: negotiate (target ≤ 2.5x verified SDE); vendor finance or earn-out; 4–8 weeks of handover training in the contract.
- Gate: verified SDE ≥ the asking price ÷ 3; ≥70% of revenue comes from sites with written agreements.

### E/F. Franchise or placement package
- Days 0–30: get the disclosure document or package contract; call 3+ existing franchisees or buyers (find your own, not just the references supplied).
- Days 31–60: visit the "secured" sites; count foot traffic; check for competing machines.
- Days 61–90: lawyer review (08); negotiate a re-site guarantee and warranty.
- Gate: the sites' expected revenue meets the A-unit test; the cooling-off period and exit terms are acceptable.

## Step 6: Conflict-resolution rules for the team
1. **Local beats global.** Melbourne or Australian data beats US data. US data is used only as a cross-check.
2. **Verified beats claimed.** Telemetry or BAS beats broker listings, which beat vendor blogs.
3. **Conservative case first.** Models use the 25th-percentile site, not the hero site.
4. When two agents disagree by more than 30% on a key number, show both values and the reasons. The Lead picks a base case and keeps the other as a sensitivity.
5. Agent 09's objections must each get either a mitigation or a "risk accepted" line in the synthesis.
