# Case Studies: How Australian Operators Start and Scale

Owner: 00 Lead Strategist · Researched 4 Nov 2026 · AUD.

> Evidence quality: **low to medium.** Most of this comes from media profiles and broker listings, seen via search-result extracts (page fetching was blocked in this session). No case study has audited financials. Numbers are claims made by the subjects or brokers.

## 1. Haven Sun: Sydney teen, 5 machines (side hustle)
- Learned the industry from YouTube and built a fleet of **5 machines**. One machine netted almost **$12k in a year** (~$1k a month).
- He keeps it as a side hustle alongside a job. His view: "you need to do at least **50 machines** to make serious money."
- In the same article, an Australian operator puts typical figures at **~$650–$750 gross and ~$400–$500 net per machine per month**. A Sydney supplier quotes combo machines at **$6,890–$8,790** new.
- Source: realcommercial.com.au, "'Not a snack machine': Twist in Aussie side hustle", https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/sad-reality-of-teens-12k-side-hustle (date not confirmed; likely 2024–25).
- **Lesson:** a hero machine is not the average. At 1–5 machines this is pocket money, not a living. The 1 → 5 stage is a learning stage.

## 2. Andres Vending (AVM): Sydney, one-man business, 46 machines
- Founder Andre-Christian Collett, 24 at the time. **46 machines** across Parramatta, Homebush and Chatswood, run from a base in Waterloo, with more being added.
- Reported "**just shy of $1 million in cash flow**" over **4 years** of operating. Aims for **1,000 machines**.
- Bottled water and chocolate are the best sellers. Chocolate peaks in winter.
- Source: realcommercial.com.au, https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/want-to-have-1000-machines-24yearold-entrepreneur-behind-sydney-vending-machine-empire
- **ESTIMATE:** $1M over 4 years with an average fleet of ~25 machines works out to ~$830 per machine per month, consistent with case 1's gross figures. "Cash flow" probably means gross takings, not profit.
- **Lesson:** one person can run ~40–50 machines if the sites are clustered geographically. That looks like the practical ceiling before you need staff or a second van.

## 3. LINK Melbourne listing: 62 machines, trading since 2008
- 62 food machines across Melbourne and the suburbs. Revenue over **$95k a month** (~$1,530 per machine per month). **65–69% gross margin.** Stock includes snacks, drinks, PPE and fresh food. "$1.2M asset value." Marked **under offer** (Jan 2025).
- Source: https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/under-offer-expansive-network-of-62-food-vending-machines-rev-95k-p-m/746904 ; https://commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/expansive-network-of-62-food-vending-machines-rev-95k-p-m-2019361013
- **Lesson:** a mature Melbourne route built over 15+ years can average about twice the revenue per machine of a beginner. Good sites compound. It also shows that **PPE and fresh food** are already part of the mix at industrial and workplace sites.

## 4. Brisbane listing: 60 machines, 36 sites, $600k WIWO
- All machines owned. Run from a home office and garage. Delivery van and a new coffee machine included. Expansion potential along the Brisbane–Ipswich corridor. Revenue **not disclosed**.
- Source: https://linkbusiness.com.au/business-for-sale/br01993/
- **ESTIMATE:** at a 2.5x multiple, $600k implies the seller claims ~$240k SDE (~$4k SDE per machine per year, ~$333 a month). That's consistent with case 1's net figures. Use this as a pricing benchmark for archetype D.

## 5. Melbourne 11-machine route: $100k ex GST
- 11 machines, rolling site contracts, operating 15+ years. Source: https://bsale.com.au/listing/rare-opportunity-for-melbourne-vic-671667
- **ESTIMATE:** $9.1k per machine. If the multiple is 2.5x, that implies ~$40k SDE a year, or ~$300 per machine per month. That would mean a 2–3 hour-a-week owner-operator business earning ~$40k. Verify against the bank statements.

## 6. Ausbox Vending Group: Melbourne, since 2004 (operator turned package seller)
- Started in Melbourne in 2004, testing snack and drink, coffee, honour-box and candy vending. It now sells "Credit Card Vending Machine Business" licence packages (~$35–37k for an Altona territory), aimed at offices, government and warehouses.
- Sources: https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business/ausbox-vending/9547 ; https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/business-listing/credit-card-vending-machine-business-ausbox-vending-group-altona-350-staff/709307
- **Lesson:** one scaling path is to **sell the system** (packages and licences) instead of running more machines. That's also a warning: some of what's on the market is sold by people who earn more from selling packages than from vending.

## 7. Provender: national unattended-retail franchise
- Covers vending, micro-markets and coffee. Franchisees are "mobile". Training happens at its Silverwater (Sydney) warehouse. Resale listings: Geelong vending from $40k + GST, Dandenong micro-market from $70k + GST, Brisbane micro-market from $140k + GST.
- Sources: https://www.seekbusiness.com.au/franchise/provender/3097 ; https://www.commercialrealestate.com.au/business-for-sale/21052-premium-micro-market-unattended-retail-franchise-business-dandenong-2020004398
- **Lesson:** franchise prices show what a franchisor-supported site is "worth". Treat them as a ceiling for how much to pay for support.

## 8. Morsl: micro-market pioneer (NSW/VIC)
- Says it is "Australia's first unattended micro-market operator", serving 5,000+ employees across NSW and VIC with 24/7 scan-and-pay kiosks.
- Source: https://yespress.io/morsl (aggregator, unverified)
- **Lesson:** micro-markets in Australia are run by B2B workplace-amenity providers, not hobbyists. You compete on service level and food quality, not on the price of the machine.

## Synthesised scaling pattern (1 → 5 → 20 → 50)

| Stage | Typical machines | What the founder is really doing | Typical capital (ESTIMATE) | Owner time (ESTIMATE) | Key milestone to move on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Test | 1–3 | Learning products, pricing, servicing; testing one site type | $3k–$12k (used) | 2–5 h/week | 3 months of telemetry data; ≥1 site above $600/month gross |
| Validate | 4–10 | Finding a repeatable site type; building a route loop | $15k–$50k | 5–12 h/week | Average ≥ $500/month gross per machine; re-site the bottom 20% |
| Route | 10–25 | Building a weekly route, wholesale accounts, storage | $50k–$150k | 15–30 h/week (part-time job level) | Owner earns ≥ a minimum wage equivalent per hour |
| Business | 25–50 | Full-time owner-operator; van; maybe casual help | $150k–$400k (or buy a route) | 35–50 h/week | Hire a route driver; corporate contracts |
| Company | 50+ | Staff, warehouse, micro-markets, sales team | $400k+ | Management | Valuation multiple ≥ 2.5x SDE |

Basis for these figures: per-machine capital of $3–8k (archetype A), the time claims in listings ("1 hour per week for 11 machines" is marketing; **ESTIMATE** 45–90 minutes per machine per week including shopping and driving), and the case 2 ceiling of ~46 machines for one person. **Hand-off to 06/07 to validate.**

## Cross-case lessons
1. **Location beats machine.** Every source agrees, and case 3's revenue per machine is twice the beginner level because of site quality.
2. **Clustering is the scale lever.** Case 2 runs one city area. Drive time is the hidden cost.
3. **Small fleets earn side income.** Five machines ≈ $12k–$30k a year net at best (ESTIMATE from cases 1 and 5).
4. **Package sellers are an industry inside the industry.** Separate operators from vendors.
5. **Workplaces and industrial sites dominate mature Melbourne routes** (cases 3 and 6). Retail centres can evict machines (case 9).
6. **No independently verified Australian P&L was found.** This is the largest evidence gap.
