# Failure Modes — Why Small Vending Operators Fail

Owner: 09 Devil's Advocate · Researched: 4 Nov 2026 · Greater Melbourne, AUD

Each failure mode lists: mechanism → evidence → which business-model options are most exposed → early warning sign.

Model options referenced (from BRIEF.md): **A** classic snack/drink machines, **B** smart fridges, **C** micro-markets, **D** niche/specialty vending, **E** buying an existing route, **F** franchise/package.

---

## 1. The location is bad (the #1 killer)

- **Mechanism:** Revenue = foot traffic × buying intent × lack of alternatives. Machines in low-traffic or "nice but not hungry" sites sell a few items a day and never pay back.
- **Evidence:**
  - Operator guidance: a machine "earns money based on one thing: foot traffic combined with buying intent" — https://www.vendingmachinesaustralia.com.au/why-location-matters-more-than-the-machine-the-real-strategy-behind-profitable-used-vending-machines (vendor source).
  - Reddit operator: "if I only had the repair shop one I'd probably be breaking even or losing money" (via search summary; mirror sites, unverified).
  - Industry rule of thumb quoted in Australian media: aim for **minimum $160/week per machine** (RealCommercial, https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/want-to-have-1000-machines-24yearold-entrepreneur-behind-sydney-vending-machine-empire).
- **Exposed:** A, D most; B/C less (they're only placed in captive workplaces).
- **Warning sign:** <$100/week gross after the first 8 weeks (novelty period).

## 2. The location disappears (host cancellation)

- **Mechanism:** Most site agreements are informal or terminable at short notice. Venues close, get sold, renovate, change manager, sign an exclusive with a big operator, or start tendering.
- **Evidence:**
  - Sydney operator Andre-Christian Collett paid **$20,000 for a site that shut down before it had earned $16,000** (RealCommercial, link above).
  - Forum buyer purchased 8 machines with no written contract; locations pulled ~30 days later when the host bid out its vending — machines went to storage (https://vendiscuss.net/topic/14920-what-questions-should-i-ask-re-purchasing-this-full-line-route/).
  - r/vendingmachines post title about losing a "$20k-a-month location" (detail unavailable).
- **Exposed:** E (route value evaporates), A, F (180-day guarantee packages).
- **Warning sign:** No written agreement; venue manager not the decision-maker; head office "reviewing suppliers".

## 3. Unit economics are thinner than sellers say

- **Mechanism:** Sellers quote *gross* sales or *gross margin* (sale price − product cost). Real net must also subtract GST, commission, card fees, telemetry, fuel/vehicle, shrink/expiry, repairs, insurance, and the operator's own time.
- **Evidence:**
  - Australian benchmarks in media: average gross sales **~$650–750/month**, net **~$400–500/month** per machine; profit 30–50% of sales; **~2.5 years to pay off** (RealCommercial, https://www.realcommercial.com.au/news/sad-reality-of-teens-12k-side-hustle — source of the estimate not identified).
  - Sydney teen with 5 machines: best machine netted ~$12k in a year, an outlier; his own view: "to actually make real money in this industry, you need to do at least **50 vending machines**" (same article).
  - US supplier: gross margins 40–60%, net 20–30% (https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/vending-machine-profit-margin — US).
- **Exposed:** All. See hidden-costs.md worked example: a "$160/week" machine nets roughly $1k/yr before labour (ESTIMATE).
- **Warning sign:** Your modelled hourly wage (net ÷ hours incl. driving) is below the Victorian minimum wage.

## 4. Buying a route that doesn't transfer

- **Mechanism:** The value of a route is its location contracts. Many are verbal, in the seller's personal name, non-assignable, or terminable on change of ownership. The seller's personal relationship with the venue manager doesn't transfer.
- **Evidence:** Route-buying guidance warns of "no exclusive agreements" letting another operator move in; r/vending buyer flagged "no formal contracts for locations" on a current listing (https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/vending-machine-routes-for-sale-what-you-need-to-know-before-buying ; search summary of r/vending).
- **Exposed:** E.
- **Warning sign:** Seller reluctant to introduce you to venues before settlement.

## 5. Scam or overpriced package

See scams-and-red-flags.md. The ACCC's Michigan Group action alleged placement in major supermarkets that had never been agreed (https://www.accc.gov.au/node/14733). **Exposed:** F, A (packaged), D (novelty machines).

## 6. Vandalism, break-ins and theft

- **Mechanism:** Unattended cash and product in public or semi-public areas attract break-ins; screens and card readers get smashed. One incident can wipe out a year's profit on that machine.
- **Evidence:**
  - Man arrested for allegedly breaking into **nine vending machines at Sydney train stations** over three months (https://www.theleader.com.au/story/5095049/man-arrested-for-series-of-vending-machine-break-ins/ — year not confirmed).
  - Return and Earn reverse vending machine in Dubbo vandalised: smashed screen, damaged panels (https://www.dailyliberal.com.au/story/5203519/vandals-attack-reverse-vending-machine-at-victoria-park/).
  - I could not find Melbourne-specific vending crime data (open question).
- **Exposed:** A, D in public/outdoor/after-hours sites (train stations, laundromats, car washes, 24/7 gyms). B/C in workplaces: lower vandalism, but see §7.
- **Warning sign:** Site is unsupervised after hours, no CCTV, previous machine was removed.

## 7. Shrinkage in smart fridges / micro-markets

- **Mechanism:** Open-shelf or honour-based formats lose stock to theft and scanning errors; smart fridges reduce but don't eliminate loss (card pre-auth failures, swapped items).
- **Evidence:** Micro-market shrink typically **4–6%** (Automatic Merchandiser 2025 survey via https://www.shekel.ai/blog/the-complete-guide-to-shrinkage-in-unattended-retail-spaces-ai-powered-solutions-for-micro-markets-and-smart-coolers-in-2025/); Cantaloupe says 3–4% typical and ~10% without deterrents (https://www.cantaloupe.com/articles/best-practices-for-securing-your-micro-market/); one car-dealership micro-market hit **12%** theft (https://www.vendingmarketwatch.com/micro-market/news/55248600/). All US/vendor sources.
- **Exposed:** C most, B moderate.
- **Warning sign:** Shrink >5% for two consecutive months.

## 8. Card-fee and payment-cost creep

- **Mechanism:** Cashless is now essential, but each sale carries a fixed + % fee from the telemetry/payment provider, plus monthly per-device fees. On a $3 item, a 17c fixed fee alone is ~5.7%.
- **Evidence:**
  - Australian forum report: reader ~$400, **$15+/month per unit, 17c per transaction** (Nayax; https://vendiscuss.net/topic/36024-any-australians-upgraded-to-card-readers/ — anecdotal).
  - US reseller pricing: US$7.95/mo + 3.5% + US$0.20 per transaction (https://vmfsusa.com/nayax-vending-card-reader-guide/).
  - **RBA surcharge ban from 1 October 2026** on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa (and reportedly Amex), with lower interchange caps (debit 8c or 0.16%; consumer credit 0.3%) — https://rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-10.html ; https://www.nab.com.au/business/payments-and-merchants/merchant-support-centre/rba-surcharging-changes. **Implication:** an operator can no longer add a card surcharge at the machine; payment costs must be absorbed in price. Whether lower interchange is passed through by vending-payment aggregators is **unknown**.
- **Exposed:** All, worst for low-price items (single snacks, cans).
- **Warning sign:** Payment costs >6% of gross sales.

## 9. Inflation, cost of living and price ceilings

- **Mechanism:** Wholesale product costs rise faster than customers accept vending price rises; customers trade down to supermarkets. Vending's convenience premium has a ceiling.
- **Evidence:** Australian annual CPI reported at **4.3% in September 2026** with food among the larger contributors (https://propertyinvestmentprofessionals.com.au/research-insights/abs-cpi-september-2026-inflation-4-3-percent-property-investors — secondary source, verify against ABS 6401.0).
- **Exposed:** A, B (snack/drink staples). D less (premium/novelty).
- **Warning sign:** Unit sales fall >10% after a price rise.

## 10. Competitor undercutting and big-operator lock-out

- **Mechanism:** The best sites (hospitals, universities, large employers, councils, transport) are tendered and held by established operators with service teams, multi-site contracts and volume supply pricing. A newcomer competes for marginal sites or undercuts on commission.
- **Evidence:** Commission is negotiated on traffic and demand; high-traffic sites demand higher commission (https://vendingexchange.com/vending-machine-locators/vending-location-commission-explained). Hand-off to 02-competitor-analyst for Melbourne incumbents.
- **Exposed:** A, B, C.
- **Warning sign:** Host asks you to match another operator's commission offer.

## 11. Operator burnout / time underestimated

- **Mechanism:** "1 hour per week" claims ignore driving in Melbourne traffic, parking, stock purchasing, expiry checks, cleaning, jams, refund requests, bookkeeping, BAS, and finding new sites. Side-hustlers quit when the hourly return is low.
- **Evidence:** See hidden-costs.md §2 time budget (ESTIMATE).
- **Exposed:** A, B, D with geographically spread sites.

## 12. Equipment failure and downtime

- **Mechanism:** Compressors, coin mechs, note validators, spiral motors and readers fail; parts and technicians are slow; a fridge machine that fails in summer spoils stock.
- **Evidence:** Downtime discussions common in operator communities (search summaries; no hard AU data).
- **Exposed:** Used machines (A) most.

## 13. Compliance surprises

- Victorian Food Act: vending machines must be registered/notified via FoodTrader; class 2/3 machines (e.g. perishable food) attract council fees and renewals; class 4 (pre-packaged shelf-stable) is notification only, no fee (https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/registration-notification-of-a-food-vending-machine-banyule-city-council/31633). Fresh food in smart fridges may push you into a higher class with inspections. Hand-off to 08.
