# Scams and Red Flags — Vending "Business Opportunities"

Owner: 09 Devil's Advocate · Researched: 4 Nov 2026 · Context: Greater Melbourne, AUD

> Research limits: web search only (direct page fetches to realcommercial.com.au, seekbusiness.com.au and businessforsale.com.au were blocked by the session network). Facts below are taken from search-result summaries of those pages; re-verify by opening the URLs before relying on exact wording.

## 1. Why vending attracts scams

Vending is sold as "passive income" — buy a box, someone else finds the site, cash rolls in. The product the beginner actually needs (a *profitable location* with a written agreement) is invisible, hard to verify, and easy to fake. That asymmetry is the core of every vending scam:

- The **machine** is a commodity with a knowable price.
- The **location** is the asset that makes money, and the seller controls all information about it.
- **Revenue projections** are unverifiable until months after the money is paid.

## 2. Documented precedent

| Case | Jurisdiction | What was promised | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Michigan Group Pty Ltd / Imobiliare Pty Ltd (from ~1998) | Australia (ACCC) | "Businesses" built on commercial orange-juicing machines to be installed in nominated major supermarket chains and fruit outlets | ACCC alleged no alliance with any major supermarket or large fruit store existed, and the business required far more investor effort than represented | https://www.accc.gov.au/node/14733 |
| Seven charged, SDNY (2015) | USA | Newspaper ads claiming "high-profit locations" available for vending machines | Machines generated little business; victims lost nearly all of their investment; locations were not pre-arranged | https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/seven-individuals-charged-manhattan-federal-court-connection-multimillion-dollar |
| FTC business-opportunity cases | USA | Couple paid US$35,000 on a promise of netting US$7,500/month; received a list of 25 "locations" | Classic locator-list fraud | https://www.ftc.gov/node/39939 (and related FTC nodes 40745, 41359, 39659) |
| Pyramid/business scheme complaints | Australia | — | 259 complaints to ACCC in 2015 about pyramid schemes, $951,721 lost (secondary source) | https://moneywiseglobal.financialknowledgecentre.com.au/kcarticles.php?id=1049 |

**Key point:** the Australian ACCC case and the US prosecutions share one mechanism — *a promise of placement in a named, high-traffic venue that the promoter had no agreement with*. Treat any claim of "we have relationships with [major chain / university / hospital network]" as false until the venue confirms it in writing.

## 4. Grey-zone (legal but bad-value) offers seen in Melbourne listings

Not every overpriced package is a scam. Many are lawful but structurally weighted to the seller. Patterns visible in late-2025/2026 Australian listings:

## 5. Red-flag checklist (use on every offer)

Score 1 point per "yes". **3+ points = walk away.** (Threshold is an ESTIMATE / judgement call.)

1. Income is described as "passive", "guaranteed" or in hours-per-week that look impossible (<15 min per machine per week incl. travel).
2. Location is promised but the venue's name, address and a contact person are not disclosed **before** payment.
3. Location guarantee is time-limited (e.g. 90/180 days) and shorter than the payback period.
4. Revenue figures are national averages, "similar machines", or projections — not 12+ months of machine-level telemetry/bank records for the actual site.
5. Pressure: deposit to "hold" a location, "last machine", price rises at end of week.
6. Machine price is >1.5× the same model bought direct (compare with 05-equipment-tech pricing).
7. Seller refuses to let you call the venue manager directly, or insists all contact goes through them.
8. No written site agreement in **your** name (or assignable to you), or the agreement is between the seller and the venue only.
9. Seller earns more from selling packages than from operating machines (ask: "how many machines do *you* operate yourself?").
10. Vague company identity — check ABN at https://abr.business.gov.au, check ASIC registers, search "[name] + scam / complaint".
11. "Exclusive territory" or "licence" fees for a non-franchise product (if it walks like a franchise, the Franchising Code may apply — hand-off to 08).
12. Machine has no serial plate, manufacturer warranty or manual (pattern noted in https://malwaretips.com/blogs/?p=408201).
13. Seller says card readers / telemetry are "included" but monthly fees then flow to the seller's own merchant account.

## 6. Due-diligence script (before any money changes hands)

1. Obtain venue name and address → visit unannounced at two different times; count foot traffic and look at existing machines.
2. Call the venue manager yourself. Ask: "Have you agreed to host a machine from [seller]? For how long? What commission? Can you terminate?"
3. Get the **site agreement draft** in your name. Have 08-legal review: term, termination notice, exclusivity, commission basis (gross vs net, inc/ex GST), power cost, access hours, insurance requirement.
4. For an existing route: demand 12–24 months of telemetry exports (Nayax/Cantaloupe/Vendon), bank deposits, and supplier invoices; reconcile them. Ask for a 30–60 day hand-over period with the seller's assistance and a holdback (part of the price paid only after locations are retained).
5. Price the machine independently (new and used).
6. Never pay a "locator fee" in full upfront; if used at all, pay on install with a clawback if the site is lost within 6–12 months. (Recommendation — ESTIMATE of fair terms, not a market norm I could verify.)
7. Report suspicious offers to Scamwatch (https://www.scamwatch.gov.au) and Consumer Affairs Victoria.

## 7. Legal hooks (hand-off to 08-legal-compliance)

- Australian Consumer Law: misleading or deceptive conduct (s18) and false representations about profitability — the basis of the ACCC Michigan Group action.
- Franchising Code of Conduct may apply to some "licence" / "system" vending packages; I could not verify where the line falls for placement packages.
- Unfair contract terms regime applies to small business contracts (penalties since Nov 2023) — relevant to one-sided site/locator agreements.
